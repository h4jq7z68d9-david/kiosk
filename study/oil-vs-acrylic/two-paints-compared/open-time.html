Two Paints Compared

Open Time

Open time is how long paint stays workable on the canvas: acrylic is dry to the touch in minutes to a few hours, oil in about two days.

Look at

  • The fingers and the folds of the blue cloth have soft edges, with no crisp outline where one shape meets the next.
  • Researchers at the National Gallery of Art trace that blur to strokes laid into paint that was still wet, which oil's long open time allows.

Claude Monet

Stacks of Wheat (End of Summer), 1890–91

Monet used both timings: the outline of the bigger stack was worked wet into wet, and in the sky more color went on once earlier strokes had dried.

Because oil cures so slowly, painters are advised to wait six months or more before varnishing an oil painting.
