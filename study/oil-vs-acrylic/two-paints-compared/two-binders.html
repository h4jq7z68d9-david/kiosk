Two Paints Compared

Two Binders

In oil paint the pigment is carried by a plant oil that hardens on contact with air; in acrylic it is mixed with a cloud of tiny plastic droplets suspended in water.

  • Behind the easel, two young assistants bend over a small table under the stairs, grinding colors for the painter.
  • The nearer assistant works at a small pot with both hands; the one behind him bends over the same table with a tool raised.

Pieter Bruegel the Elder

The Harvesters, 1565

The same oil serves for washes so thin that the drawing underneath still shows and for strokes thick enough to hide it.

Water thins acrylic and cleans its brushes; oil's solvents call for ventilation, and oily rags, which can heat up and ignite by themselves, belong in water in a closed can or a metal fireproof bin.
