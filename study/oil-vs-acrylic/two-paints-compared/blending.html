Two Paints Compared

Blending

Blending means working two colors into each other while both are still wet, so the change from one to the other is gradual.

Look at

  • The brown trees thin into the pale sky in soft, broken patches, without a firm outline.
  • The National Gallery of Art notes that Leonardo used his hands to blend where the sky meets the juniper; his fingerprints are still in the paint.

Pietro Rotari

A Girl with a Flower in Her Hair, 1760–62

The National Gallery of Art reports delicate blended brushstrokes in her face, with the flower and her features finished last.

To keep acrylic workable, painters mist the palette or add a little retarder; overdoing the retarder gives a soft paint film that can stay tacky.
