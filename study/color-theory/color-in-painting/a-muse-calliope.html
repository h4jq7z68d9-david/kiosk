Color in Painting

Cosimo Tura

A Muse (Calliope?)

Probably 1455–60

Oil over egg tempera on wood (poplar), 116.2 × 71.1 cm. The National Gallery, London

A crimson mantle, a dark blue dress, a green skirt and red sleeves shot with gold glow from layered paint. Oil was still uncommon in Italy in the 1450s, and here it carries the final layers of color.

  • Under the oil lies an earlier, partly finished design in egg tempera.
  • The richest passages are built up in translucent oil layers over more solid paint, an effect beyond egg tempera.
  • For pale colors he chose walnut oil, the less yellow of the two oils he used, to keep them bright.
