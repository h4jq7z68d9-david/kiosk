Color in Painting

Edvard Munch

The Scream

1893

Tempera and crayon on cardboard, 91 × 73.5 cm. Nasjonalmuseet, Oslo

Bands of red, orange and yellow fill the sky above the fjord, and the same curving strokes run through land, water and figure. The museum calls the colors non-realistic and ties them to the subject, anxiety.

  • In notebook lines written before the painting, Munch recalled a sunset sky turning blood red, with flames over the blue-black fjord.
  • A year earlier he used the same colors, format and landscape in Sick Mood at Sunset; chiefly the central figure changed.
  • In the later painted version at the Munch Museum, cadmium yellow strokes in the sky have faded to off-white, mainly from damp, not light.

Munch also made a black-and-white lithograph of the motif in 1895; some impressions were colored by hand in watercolor.
