Light, Paint and the Eye

Mixing Light and Mixing Paint

Mixing light adds colors together; mixing paint takes light away, so paint mixtures turn darker and duller.

Look at

  • Gamboge, a yellow, and Prussian blue each return a wide band of the spectrum, shown beside their swatches.
  • Stirred together as paint, each pigment swallows what the other returns, and only the green band they share is left.
  • Mixed as light, the same two keep both bands and come out a pale gray instead of green.

Titian

Bacchus and Ariadne, 1520–23

Titian laid his pigments on nearly pure and lightened them only with white, so the ultramarine sky keeps the strength a mixture would lose.
