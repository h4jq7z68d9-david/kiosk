Light, Paint and the Eye

Simultaneous Contrast

A color changes with what surrounds it, so the same paint looks different on different grounds.

Look at

  • One ornament, printed in a single light ink, sits on six colored grounds.
  • On the yellow ground it almost disappears; on the dark red it stands out nearly white.

Vincent van Gogh

Irises, 1889

Van Gogh set blue-violet irises against orange flowers and green leaves against red earth, so each color makes its neighbor look stronger.

Chevreul listed the shifts: gray patterns look greenish on rose, pink on green, lilac on yellow and orange-gray on blue.
