The Academy's Rules

From the 1600s, academies and writers set rules for good pictures.

The French Royal Academy, founded in 1648, ranked subjects from history painting down to still life.

  • In 1783 Reynolds described blotting Venetian pictures: no more than a quarter light, a quarter dark, half in between.
  • In 1797 J. T. Smith coined a "rule of thirds": over two-thirds in shadow, and sky, land or water split about 2 to 1.
  • He meant a ratio of areas and lines, not today's grid of nine equal boxes.
