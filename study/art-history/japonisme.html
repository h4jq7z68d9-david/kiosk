Japonisme

Japanese prints, props and design in Western art

Japonisme is the French name for a passion for the art and design of Japan that took hold in France and spread through Europe and North America after Japan resumed trade with the West in the 1850s. The critic Philippe Burty is generally credited with the word, in the early 1870s. Some artists painted fans, kimono and prints as studio props; others borrowed what the woodblock prints did with form: flat, bright color, firm outlines, long or tall formats, views from above, figures cut by the edge and wide empty areas.
