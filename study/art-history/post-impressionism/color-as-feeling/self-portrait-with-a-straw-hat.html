Color as Feeling

Vincent van Gogh

Self-Portrait with a Straw Hat

1887

Oil on canvas, 40.6 × 31.8 cm. The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

A small head-and-shoulders view painted in Paris. It shows how quickly his dark Dutch manner gave way to light and color.

  • Short, separate strokes of color build the face, hat and background. The Met links this to his growing grasp of Neo-Impressionist methods and color theory.
  • Look at the hat brim, where yellow strokes are laid in curving, radiating rows.
  • Bare canvas shows between the strokes in many places, especially in the background.
  • He painted it on the reverse of a peasant study from 1885, The Potato Peeler.
  • It came to the Met in 1967 through the bequest of Adelaide Milton de Groot.

In Paris, from 1886 to 1888, he painted over twenty self-portraits. Short of money for models, he worked from a mirror and used himself instead.
