Color as Feeling

Vincent van Gogh

The Night Café

1888

Oil on canvas, 72.4 × 92.1 cm. Yale University Art Gallery, New Haven

A few drinkers slump at tables in a harshly lit room at night. He meant the place to feel like somewhere a person could go to ruin.

  • Blood-red walls clash with a green billiard table and ceiling. As he told Theo, the clash was meant to express dark human passions rather than describe the room.
  • Look at the lamps, each ringed by halos of yellow and orange strokes.
  • He studied gaslight's effect: the owner's white clothes shift toward lemon yellow and pale green.
  • The title is painted below the signature at lower right.
  • Once owned by Ivan Morozov in Moscow, it came to Yale in 1961 by Stephen Carlton Clark's bequest.

It was the Café de l'Alcazar in Arles, an all-night haunt of drifters and prostitutes. He took his meals there.
