Dream, Decoration and the Wider World

Walter Sickert

Gatti's Hungerford Palace of Varieties. Second Turn of Katie Lawrence

c. 1888

Oil on canvas, 38.7 × 47 cm. Yale University Art Gallery, New Haven

A singer in a pale dress stands alone in the footlights, under a gilded arch hung with red. The crowd below sits in shadow.

  • Degas and Manet had made the café-concert a worthy subject in Paris, and the young painter knew Degas. Here that city theme moves to a London variety stage.
  • He first showed at the New English Art Club with a picture of this act, and critics called the subject vulgar.
  • Look at the man in a bow tie in the front row, facing out while the rest watch the stage.
  • He sketched in the halls almost nightly, and paintings like this grew from those sketches.
  • Yale acquired it through the Mary Gertrude Abbey Fund.

The singer was a popular star of the halls, later known for making Daisy Bell a hit. Her name survives mostly through his pictures of her.
