Moulin Rouge: La Goulue

1891

Lithograph printed in four colors on three sheets, 190 × 116.5 cm. The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

A dancer kicks high above a crowd of black silhouettes on a street poster. This advertisement launched the artist's career as a poster designer.

  • A painter's eye is put to work selling a dance hall: flat color, bold lettering and a cropped crowd. Art and advertising now shared the same street wall.
  • The dance hall commissioned the poster.
  • Look for the gray dancer in front, Valentin le Désossé, whose stage name means boneless.
  • The Paris printer Affiches Américaines, of Charles Lévy, produced the sheets.
  • The Met acquired this complete impression in 1932.

The hall first opened its doors in 1889, on the boulevard de Clichy. Its star dancer went by a nickname meaning the glutton.

Color lithograph
A print made by drawing each color on its own stone, then printing the stones in turn on one sheet.
