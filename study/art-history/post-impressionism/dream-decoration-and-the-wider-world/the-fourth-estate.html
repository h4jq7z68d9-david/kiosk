Dream, Decoration and the Wider World

Giuseppe Pellizza da Volpedo

The Fourth Estate

1898–1901

Oil on canvas, 283 × 550 cm. Galleria d'Arte Moderna (GAM), Milan

A broad line of working men and women strides out of shadow toward the viewer. On a huge canvas, a workers' march takes on the scale of history painting.

  • Here the divisionist method serves a political subject rather than a landscape. In Italy, painters of the group used effects of light to heighten feeling.
  • The 1902 Turin Quadriennale showed it first, and it did not sell there.
  • Look at the woman beside the two leading men, carrying a baby as she steps forward.
  • It grew out of about a decade of studies and three earlier versions.
  • Milan bought it by public subscription in 1920.

The museum says the final title drew on Jean Jaurès's writings on the French Revolution, casting working people as a fourth estate. It was painted in years of strikes, protests and workers' demands.
