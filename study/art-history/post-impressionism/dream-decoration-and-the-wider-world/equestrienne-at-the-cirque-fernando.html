Dream, Decoration and the Wider World

Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec

Equestrienne (At the Cirque Fernando)

1887–88

Oil on canvas, 100.3 × 161.3 cm. The Art Institute of Chicago

A trick rider circles a circus ring while the ringmaster urges her horse on. It is an early large painting of Montmartre entertainment, made before the posters.

  • Faces lean toward exaggeration: the rider's heavy makeup and snarl, the ringmaster's answering glare. Pushing features this far sharpens character instead of flattering the sitter.
  • Joseph Oller, owner of the Moulin Rouge, bought it to hang in his dance hall.
  • Look at the ringmaster's long whip, curving across the empty floor toward the horse.
  • The ring's curve sweeps diagonally across the canvas, carrying the eye along with the horse.
  • In 1925 the Art Institute purchased it from Paul Rosenberg & Co.

The Cirque Fernando was a Montmartre circus that Renoir also painted. Suzanne Valadon may have modeled for the rider.
