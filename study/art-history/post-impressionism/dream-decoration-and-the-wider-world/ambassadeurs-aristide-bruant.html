Dream, Decoration and the Wider World

Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec

Ambassadeurs: Aristide Bruant

1892

Color lithograph on tan wove paper, 147.2 × 99.9 cm. The Art Institute of Chicago

A cabaret singer in a wide black hat and red scarf turns to glare past the viewer. The poster sells a performer's stage persona as much as his show.

  • The figure is cut down to a few flat shapes: hat, cloak and scarf. Posters like this traded modeled detail for shapes that read at once from across a street.
  • The singer commissioned it himself to advertise his shows at an upscale club.
  • Look at the low viewpoint, which makes the figure loom over the passer-by.
  • The design was printed in five colors, with the scarf one flat red.
  • It came to the Art Institute in the Mr. and Mrs. Carter H. Harrison Collection.

The singer played a rough outsider who mocked upper-class audiences at his own cabaret, Le Mirliton. The artist reused and adapted this image in later designs.
