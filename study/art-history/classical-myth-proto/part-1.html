Myth and Its World

The Nature of Myth

What myth is, the kinds of myth, and the Olympian gods

A myth is a traditional story that a whole community treats as important. It has a plot, characters in conflict, and a setting in the distant past, and it was passed along by word of mouth long before anyone wrote it down. Because every teller changed the details, Greek myths never had one official version. This section introduces the main kinds of myth and the twelve Olympian gods who drive most of the stories that later artists painted and carved.

  • Myth = story with a plot, conflict, and a far-off past
  • Three kinds: divine myth, legend, folktale
  • Greek gods kept by Rome under Latin names
Myth
A traditional story with importance for a whole community, not just one teller; the subject of every work on this site.
Mythos
The Greek word behind “myth,” first meaning an authoritative spoken account rather than a made-up tale.
Pantheon
The full set of a culture’s gods taken as a group, such as the Greek Olympians.
Divine myth
A story whose main actors are gods and other supernatural beings, set before human time (for example, the war of gods and Giants).
Legend (saga)
A story about human heroes, often nobles, set in real places such as Troy or Athens; it works like ancient history.
Folktale
A story about ordinary people or talking animals told to entertain and teach social rules, like Aesop’s fables.
Oral tradition
Passing stories along by memory and performance; it explains why the same myth survives in different versions.
