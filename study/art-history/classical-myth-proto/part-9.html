Demeter and Dionysus

Myths of fertility, mystery, and ecstasy

Demeter and Dionysus are the gods of grain and wine, the two foods that kept the Greek world alive. Their myths are about loss and return: Persephone taken to the underworld and restored each spring, Dionysus born twice and arriving as a stranger. Both gods offered worshippers something the other Olympians did not, secret rites and ecstatic experiences that promised a closer bond with the divine.

“I begin to sing of rich-haired Demeter, awful goddess—of her and her trim-ankled daughter whom Aidoneus rapt away”

Homeric Hymn to Demeter 1–3, trans. Hugh G. Evelyn-White (1914)
