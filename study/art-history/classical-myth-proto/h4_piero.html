Piero di Cosimo

The Myth of Prometheus

ca. 1510–15

Oil on poplar panel · Alte Pinakothek, Munich

Made for: One of a pair of long panels (the other is in Strasbourg) made as part of the furnishings of a room in a Florentine home.

Piero tells the story as a sequence: Prometheus shapes a clay statue of a man, flies up with Athena to light a torch from the sun’s chariot, and brings the fire down to give the figure life. In this version, drawn from later writers, Prometheus is less a trickster than a creator, an idea Renaissance thinkers compared to God shaping Adam.

  • Continuous narrative: one panel, several moments
  • Prometheus as maker of humans
  • Fire from the sun animates the figure
