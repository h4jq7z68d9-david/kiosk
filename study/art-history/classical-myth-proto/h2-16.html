The Cultural Context

Where the stories come from

The main written sources

Greek myths were told for centuries before Homer and Hesiod wrote versions down around 700 BCE. Athenian tragedy reworked them for the stage in the 400s BCE, and Roman poets gave them their most influential forms. The works below are the main sources later artists read.

  • Homer and Hesiod: earliest written versions (ca. 700 BCE)
  • Tragedy: myth staged in Classical Athens
  • Virgil and Ovid: the Roman retellings artists used most
Homer, Iliad and Odyssey
Epic poems; the Iliad covers weeks in the tenth year of the Trojan War, the Odyssey Odysseus’s ten-year voyage home.
Hesiod, Theogony and Works and Days
The Theogony tells the birth of the gods and Zeus’s rise; Works and Days gives advice on life and includes Prometheus, Pandora, and the Five Ages.
Tragedy: Aeschylus, Sophocles, Euripides
Athenian playwrights who staged myths at festivals of Dionysus; Aeschylus stresses the gods, Sophocles fate, Euripides human passion.
Virgil, Aeneid (29–19 BCE)
Roman epic written under Augustus, tracing Aeneas from fallen Troy to Italy and Rome’s founding.
Ovid, Metamorphoses (8 CE)
A long Roman poem retelling hundreds of myths of transformation; the single most-used source for later artists.
