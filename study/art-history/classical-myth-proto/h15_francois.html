The François Vase

ca. 570–565 BCE

Black-figure volute krater · National Archaeological Museum, Florence · shown in an early-20th-century drawing

Made for: A great wine-mixing bowl found in an Etruscan tomb at Chiusi; its bands of about 270 figures, many labeled with their names, make it a picture book of myth.

The main band shows the gods arriving at the wedding of the mortal Peleus and the sea goddess Thetis, whose son would be Achilles. At that wedding Eris, goddess of strife, who had not been invited, threw in a golden apple marked “for the fairest.” Other bands show Achilles’ later deeds and Ajax carrying his body. Because so many figures are labeled, the vase is a key document of how Greeks told these stories.
