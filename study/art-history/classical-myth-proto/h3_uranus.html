Giorgio Vasari and Cristofano Gherardi

The Mutilation of Uranus by Saturn

ca. 1556

Ceiling painting · Sala degli Elementi, Palazzo Vecchio, Florence

Made for: Part of a ceiling program for Duke Cosimo I de’ Medici’s palace apartments, using myth to celebrate the elements and, indirectly, the duke.

Uranus hated his children and kept them hidden inside Earth, so Gaea made a great sickle and her youngest Titan son, Cronus, ambushed and castrated his father. Blood falling on the earth produced the Furies and the Giants, and the severed parts thrown into the sea produced Aphrodite. Vasari’s ceiling turns this violent episode into the origin of the elements.

  • Gaea’s sickle; Cronus attacks Uranus
  • Blood → Furies, Giants
  • Sea foam → Aphrodite
Titans
The twelve children of Gaea and Uranus, including Oceanus, Hyperion, Cronus, and Rhea; the generation of gods before the Olympians.
Erinyes (Furies)
Female spirits of vengeance born from Uranus’s blood, who hunt down those who shed family blood.
Giants
Huge, violent sons of Earth born from Uranus’s blood, later defeated by the Olympians.
