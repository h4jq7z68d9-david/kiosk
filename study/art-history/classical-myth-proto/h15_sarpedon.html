Euphronios

Krater with the Death of Sarpedon

Athenian, ca. 515 BCE

Red-figure calyx krater · National Archaeological Museum of Cerveteri (formerly at the Metropolitan Museum)

Made for: A wine-mixing bowl looted from an Etruscan tomb, bought by the Metropolitan Museum in 1972 and returned to Italy in 2008.

With Achilles out of the fight, the Trojans drove the Greeks back to their ships. Patroclus put on Achilles’ armor and led a counterattack, killing Sarpedon, a son of Zeus who fought for Troy. Zeus allowed his son’s death but sent Sleep and Death to carry his body home. Euphronios shows the two winged brothers lifting the bleeding hero while Hermes watches.

  • Patroclus kills Sarpedon, son of Zeus
  • Sleep and Death carry him home
