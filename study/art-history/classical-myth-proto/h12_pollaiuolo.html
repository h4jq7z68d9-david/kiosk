Antonio del Pollaiuolo

Hercules and the Hydra

ca. 1475

Tempera on panel · Uffizi Gallery, Florence · one of a pair with Hercules and Antaeus

Made for: One of two small panels repeating large canvases of the labors made for the Medici palace in Florence, where Hercules was a civic hero.

The first labors cleared the land of monsters: the Nemean lion, whose skin Heracles wore, and the nine-headed Hydra of Lerna, which grew two heads for each one cut off until his nephew Iolaus burned the stumps. Then came the Golden Hind of Artemis, the Erymanthian Boar, the filthy stables of King Augeas, and the man-eating Stymphalian Birds. Pollaiuolo, an expert in anatomy, gives the hero taut, straining muscles against a wide Tuscan landscape.

  • Labors 1–6 in the Peloponnese
  • Hydra’s heads seared by Iolaus
  • Pollaiuolo’s study of muscles in action
Labors 1–6
The Nemean Lion, the Lernaean Hydra, the Golden Hind of Artemis, the Erymanthian Boar, the Augean Stables, and the Stymphalian Birds.
Iolaus
Heracles’ nephew and loyal companion, who helped him defeat the Hydra.
Chiron
Wise centaur accidentally wounded by Heracles during the boar hunt; he gave up his immortality.
Olympic Games
Said to have been founded by Heracles after he cleaned the Augean stables.
