Guido Reni

Aurora

1612–14

Fresco · Casino dell’Aurora, Palazzo Pallavicini-Rospigliosi, Rome

Made for: A ceiling fresco for the garden pavilion of Cardinal Scipione Borghese’s palace in Rome.

The sun god drives his chariot across the sky, led by Aurora, the dawn, scattering flowers, and circled by the dancing Hours. In the 1800s the philologist Max Müller argued that most myths began as poetic descriptions of the sun, dawn, and sky, misunderstood as language changed, so that a story like Daphne fleeing Apollo meant the dawn fleeing the sun. Solar mythology faded, but comparing myths across Indo-European languages remained influential, notably in Georges Dumézil’s theory of three social functions.
