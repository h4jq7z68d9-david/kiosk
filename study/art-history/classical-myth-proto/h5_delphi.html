Temple of Apollo at Delphi

4th century BCE (rebuilt after 373 BCE)

Limestone · Delphi, Greece, on the slopes of Mount Parnassus

Made for: The home of Apollo’s oracle, the most important in the Greek world, consulted by cities and individuals before major decisions.

Apollo was born on the island of Delos to the Titaness Leto, after Hera banned every land from receiving her and held back the birth goddess Eileithyia. As a young god he killed the serpent Python at Delphi and took over the site. His temple carried two famous mottoes, “Know yourself” and “Nothing in excess.” Anyone stained by bloodshed had to be purified before approaching him.

  • Apollo born on Delos
  • Kills the Python and claims Delphi
  • Mottoes: know yourself; nothing in excess
Delphi
Sanctuary of Apollo on Mount Parnassus, thought to be the center (navel) of the world.
Python
The serpent Apollo killed to take Delphi; the Pythia and the Pythian Games are named for it.
Telphusa
A spring nymph who tried to steer Apollo away from Delphi; he buried her spring in punishment.
Tityus
A giant who attacked Leto and was killed by Apollo and Artemis, then punished in the underworld.
Miasma
Ritual pollution, especially from bloodshed, that Apollo’s rites could cleanse.
