Frederic Leighton

The Garden of the Hesperides

1892

Oil on canvas · Lady Lever Art Gallery, Port Sunlight

Made for: A round exhibition painting shown at the Royal Academy, made for wealthy Victorian collectors.

When Hera married Zeus, Earth gave her a tree of golden apples, which grew in a garden at the western edge of the world. The nymphs called the Hesperides tended it, guarded by the serpent Ladon, near where Atlas holds up the sky. Leighton’s drowsy nymphs and coiled serpent prepare for the later labor in which Heracles takes the apples.

  • Golden apples: Earth’s wedding gift to Hera
  • Guarded by the Hesperides and Ladon
  • Later stolen in a labor of Heracles
Hera’s children
With Zeus, Hebe (youth), Eileithyia (childbirth), and Ares; in Hesiod she bears Hephaestus on her own.
Ladon
The serpent that coils around the tree of golden apples.
