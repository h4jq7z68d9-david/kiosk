After Pieter Bruegel the Elder

Landscape with the Fall of Icarus

ca. 1560s

Oil on canvas · Royal Museums of Fine Arts of Belgium, Brussels

Made for: A landscape for a collector, probably an early copy of a lost Bruegel painting.

Daedalus made wings of feathers and wax for himself and his son Icarus and warned him not to fly too high. Icarus flew toward the sun, the wax melted, and he fell into the sea. Bruegel shrinks the tragedy to a pair of legs splashing in the corner while a plowman, a shepherd, and a fisherman go on with their work, a reminder that the world takes little notice of one person’s fall.

  • Wings of feathers and wax
  • Icarus flies too high and falls
  • Tiny tragedy in a busy world
