Heroes of Athens and Thebes

Thebes, Oedipus, and Jason

A cursed city, a tragic king, and the voyage of the Argo

Thebes was a city founded in blood and haunted by curses, and its royal family produced the most famous tragedy of all: Oedipus, who killed his father and married his mother without knowing it. Jason’s quest for the Golden Fleece, by contrast, is an adventure story, though it too ends in disaster when he betrays the sorceress Medea. Athenian playwrights turned both legends into tragedies still performed today.

  • Thebes: founded by Cadmus, cursed for generations
  • Oedipus: fate, guilt, and knowledge
  • Jason and Medea: quest and betrayal
Sophocles, Oedipus the King
Tragedy (ca. 429 BCE) in which Oedipus discovers his own crimes; Aristotle’s model of tragic plot.
Aeschylus, Seven against Thebes
Tragedy (467 BCE) about the war between Oedipus’s sons.
Sophocles, Antigone
Tragedy in which Antigone buries her brother against the king’s order.
Apollonius of Rhodes, Argonautica
Hellenistic epic (3rd century BCE) of Jason’s voyage.
Euripides, Medea
Tragedy (431 BCE) in which Medea takes revenge on Jason.
