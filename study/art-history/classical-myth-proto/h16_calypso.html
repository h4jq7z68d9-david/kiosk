Arnold Böcklin

Odysseus and Calypso

1882–83

Tempera on mahogany panel · Kunstmuseum Basel

Made for: A Symbolist painting for collectors, one of Böcklin’s moody mythological landscapes.

Odysseus was washed ashore on Ogygia, island of the nymph Calypso, who kept him for seven years and offered to make him immortal. He refused, longing for home and his mortal wife. Böcklin shows him as a dark, faceless figure staring out to sea from a rock while Calypso sits apart in red. The sea goddess Leucothea later saved him from a storm on his raft.

  • Seven years with Calypso
  • Refuses immortality for home
  • Saved at sea by Leucothea
Calypso
Nymph of Ogygia who kept Odysseus for seven years.
Leucothea
The former Theban princess Ino, now a sea goddess, who gave Odysseus her veil to save him.
