Gustave Moreau

Oedipus and the Sphinx

1864

Oil on canvas · Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Made for: The painting that made Moreau famous at the Paris Salon of 1864, an early landmark of Symbolism.

Moreau’s Sphinx clings to Oedipus’s chest, a beautiful and deadly woman locked in a staring match with the hero, above the remains of earlier victims. Within a few decades Sigmund Freud would read the Oedipus myth as a key to the mind, and Carl Jung would see myths as expressions of a shared unconscious. Psychology gave myth a new meaning: stories about the hidden self.
