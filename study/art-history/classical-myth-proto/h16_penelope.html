Pinturicchio

Penelope with the Suitors

ca. 1509

Fresco, detached · National Gallery, London

Made for: Painted for a room in the Siena palace of Pandolfo Petrucci, ruler of the city, celebrating faithful wives.

At home, more than a hundred suitors had moved into Odysseus’s palace, eating his wealth and pressing Penelope to remarry. She promised to choose once she finished weaving a shroud for her father-in-law Laertes, but unraveled the work every night. Odysseus, disguised as a beggar and helped by his loyal swineherd Eumaeus and his son Telemachus, strung his great bow, which no suitor could bend, and killed them all. Pinturicchio shows Penelope at her loom as the suitors and the disguised Odysseus enter.
