Bronze god from Cape Artemision

Greek, ca. 460 BCE

Bronze · National Archaeological Museum, Athens

Made for: A large sanctuary statue lost in an ancient shipwreck and recovered from the sea in 1926–28; it may have been made as an offering to the god.

Poseidon, the Roman Neptune, rules the sea, earthquakes, and horses, and he is a brother of Zeus and Hades. This bronze once held a weapon that is now lost, so scholars still debate whether it showed Poseidon with his trident or Zeus with a thunderbolt. Either way it shows the Early Classical ideal: a perfectly balanced, mature body caught at the moment before a throw. Bronzes this size rarely survive, because most were melted down for their metal.

  • God of sea, earthquakes, horses
  • Attributes: trident, dolphin, horse, bull
  • Identity debated: Poseidon or Zeus
