Arkesilas Painter

Cup with Atlas and Prometheus

Laconian (Spartan), ca. 560–550 BCE

Black-figure terracotta · Vatican Museums (Museo Gregoriano Etrusco)

Made for: A drinking cup from Sparta, found in Etruria; the picture filled the inside of the bowl.

Two brothers suffer side by side: Atlas holds up the sky, and Prometheus, bound to a post, is attacked by Zeus’s eagle, which eats his liver every day while it grows back each night. This is one of the earliest images of the myth. In Hesiod the hero Heracles later kills the eagle and frees Prometheus, with Zeus’s consent.

  • Earliest images of the punishment
  • Eagle eats a regrowing liver
  • Atlas and Prometheus are brothers
