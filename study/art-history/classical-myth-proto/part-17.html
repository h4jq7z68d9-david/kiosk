Aeneas and the Founding of Rome

Virgil’s epic and Rome’s own legends

The Romans adopted the Greek gods but told their own foundation stories, and those stories were about duty, family, and the state. Virgil’s Aeneid, written for the emperor Augustus, makes the Trojan Aeneas the ancestor of Rome and of Augustus’s own family. Roman historians such as Livy then told of Romulus and Remus, the Sabine women, and the heroes of the early Republic.

“Arms, and the man I sing, who, forc’d by fate, And haughty Juno’s unrelenting hate, Expell’d and exil’d, left the Trojan shore.”

Virgil, Aeneid 1.1–3, trans. John Dryden (1697)

The clan sharing a family name, and the household under one father.

The male head of the household and his legal power over its members.
Genius and pietas
The life-spirit of a man or family, and the duty owed to gods, family, and country.

A Roman name for the god of the underworld.
