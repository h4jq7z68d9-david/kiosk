The Beginning of the World

Creation and the Rise of Zeus

From Chaos to the rule of the Olympians

Hesiod’s Theogony (“birth of the gods”) tells how the world began with Chaos and how three generations of gods fought for control of it. Sky-god Uranus is overthrown by his son Cronus, and Cronus by his son Zeus. Zeus then has to defeat the Titans, the monster Typhon, and the Giants before the Olympians rule for good and divide the world among themselves.

“Verily at the first Chaos came to be, but next wide-bosomed Earth”

Hesiod, Theogony 116–117, trans. Hugh G. Evelyn-White (1914)
  • Chaos → Earth → Sky → Titans → Olympians
  • Three cosmic wars secure Zeus’s rule
  • World divided: sky, sea, underworld
Chaos
The first thing to exist in Hesiod: a yawning gap or void, not disorder.
Gaea (Gaia)
Earth, who comes from Chaos and gives birth to the Sky and the Sea, then mother of the Titans and many monsters.
Uranus
The Sky, son and husband of Gaea; overthrown by his son Cronus.
Tartarus
The deepest pit beneath the earth, where defeated gods and monsters are locked away.
