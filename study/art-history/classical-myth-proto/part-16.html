Troy and the Journey Home

The Odyssey

Odysseus’s ten-year journey home

Homer’s Odyssey follows Odysseus, king of Ithaca, on his ten-year voyage home after Troy, and his wife Penelope and son Telemachus, who wait for him while suitors take over his palace. Where the Iliad celebrates strength, the Odyssey celebrates intelligence, endurance, and the right treatment of guests. Odysseus survives monsters, witches, and goddesses by his wits, then wins back his home in disguise.

“Tell me, O Muse, of that ingenious hero who travelled far and wide after he had sacked the famous town of Troy.”

Homer, Odyssey 1.1–2, trans. Samuel Butler (1900)
