Enlightenment

Jean Siméon Chardin

La Raie

1728

Oil on canvas, 45 × 57 1/2 in. Musée du Louvre, Paris

A kitchen table won its painter a place in the Academy: still life, painted well enough to serve as a reception piece.

Look at

  • The gutted ray hangs at the centre, its red flesh and pale skin one of two big light shapes, with the white cloth, against a brown stone wall.
  • The table is one long horizontal; the ray's point, the cat at left and the dark jug at right make a broad triangle on it.
  • Paint is thick and dragged in the flesh and the white cloth, thin and scumbled on the wall.
  • Kept by the Academy until the Revolution, then seized and hung in the Louvre from June 1796.
  • Shown again in the Chardin retrospectives of 1979 and 1999.
