Rococo

François Boucher

Diane sortant du bain

1742

Oil on canvas, 22 1/2 × 28 3/4 in. Musée du Louvre, Paris

A crescent moon, a bow and dead game identify Diana; what Boucher actually paints is the difference between skin, silk and fur.

Look at

  • Pink and pearl flesh against the blue drape and dark green wood makes the bodies the brightest, warmest thing on the canvas.
  • The two figures sit right of centre, one above the other, their legs crossing in diagonals; the left third is landscape and dogs.
  • Flesh is blended smooth, while pearls, ribbons and the birds' feathers are picked out in small, sharp touches.
  • Shown at the Salon of 1742; sold in 1783 with a pendant that was probably the Repos de Vénus now in Berlin.
  • Bought by the Louvre from a Paris owner, Van Cuyck, in 1852 for 3,200 francs.
