Fauvism and Early Expressionism

Colour let loose, then line and paint pushed to the edge

In 1905 a group of painters in Paris, soon nicknamed the Fauves, the wild beasts, began using colour for its own force rather than to describe things. Within a few years painters in Dresden, Munich and Vienna pushed the same freedom further, into jagged line, raw paint and, by 1913, pictures with no objects at all.
