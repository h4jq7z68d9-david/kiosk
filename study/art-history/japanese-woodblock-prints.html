Japan, 1680s–1880s

Japanese Woodblock Prints

Line, flat color and bold design in prints of the floating world

Ukiyo-e, “pictures of the floating world,” were sold as woodblock prints from the 1680s into the 1880s. They were first made in Edo, the city now called Tokyo. Publishers issued them in quantity for the city's townspeople. The most common subjects were courtesans and actors of kabuki, a theater of dance and drama; landscape came later. Each print took four people: a designer, a block carver, a printer and the publisher who issued it for sale.
