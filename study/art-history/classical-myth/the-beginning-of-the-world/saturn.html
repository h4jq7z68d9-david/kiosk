The Beginning of the World · Creation and the Rise of Zeus

Peter Paul Rubens

Saturn

1636–38

Oil on canvas · Museo del Prado, Madrid

Made for: One of dozens of mythological paintings commissioned by Philip IV of Spain for his hunting lodge, the Torre de la Parada.

Cronus, the Roman Saturn, learned that one of his children would overthrow him, so he swallowed each one at birth. His wife Rhea saved the youngest, Zeus, by hiding him on Crete and giving Cronus a stone wrapped in swaddling clothes. Rubens paints an old man biting into a child’s chest, with the stars of the planet Saturn above. Renaissance writers confused Cronus with Chronos, “Time,” which is why he often carries a scythe and appears as Father Time.

  • Cronus swallows his children
  • Rhea substitutes a stone for Zeus
  • Confused with Time (Chronos)
Cronus (Kronos)
Youngest Titan, who overthrew Uranus and swallowed his own children to keep power.
Rhea
Titaness, wife of Cronus and mother of Hestia, Demeter, Hera, Hades, Poseidon, and Zeus.

“These great Cronos swallowed as each came forth from the womb to his mother’s knees”
