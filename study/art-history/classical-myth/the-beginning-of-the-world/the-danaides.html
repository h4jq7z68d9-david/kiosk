The Beginning of the World · The Origins of Mortals

John William Waterhouse

The Danaïdes

1903

Oil on canvas · private collection

Made for: A Victorian exhibition painting of a punishment in the underworld.

The fifty daughters of Danaus were forced to marry their cousins, and all but one killed her husband on the wedding night. In the underworld they were sentenced to carry water forever in leaking jars that never fill. Waterhouse paints them pouring endlessly into a basin. The jar, like Pandora’s, links women with containers, and Greek ideas of marriage cast the bride as a vessel for children.

  • Killed their husbands on the wedding night
  • Endless water-carrying in Hades
  • Jar as symbol of the female body
Danaïds
The daughters of Danaus, punished in the underworld for murdering their husbands by carrying water in leaking jars.
Marriage = children = immortality
Greek belief that, with no happy afterlife for most people, a person lived on through children, which made marriage necessary.

Themes

  • Woman as a container
