The Beginning of the World · The Origins of Mortals

Peter Paul Rubens, eagle by Frans Snyders

Prometheus Bound

1611–18

Oil on canvas · Philadelphia Museum of Art

Made for: A large showpiece Rubens kept for years and later sold; he chose the subject to show his mastery of the heroic nude.

Rubens shows Prometheus falling headfirst down the rocks as the eagle tears at him, a pose built from his study of ancient sculpture and Michelangelo. In Aeschylus’s play Prometheus Bound, the Titan defends himself by listing what he taught humans: building, counting, writing, medicine, and more. That made him a hero of human progress for later artists and writers.

  • Collaboration with Snyders (eagle)
  • Pose from ancient sculpture and Michelangelo
  • Aeschylus: benefactor of civilization
