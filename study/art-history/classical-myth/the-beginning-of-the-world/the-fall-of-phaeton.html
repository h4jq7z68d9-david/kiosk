The Beginning of the World · Creation and the Rise of Zeus

Peter Paul Rubens

The Fall of Phaeton

ca. 1604–08

Oil on canvas · National Gallery of Art, Washington

Made for: An early large mythological painting from Rubens’s years in Rome; its first owner is not recorded.

The Titan Hyperion fathered the sun (Helios), the moon (Selene), and the dawn (Eos). Helios’s son Phaeton begged to drive the sun’s chariot for one day, lost control, and scorched the earth until Zeus struck him down with a thunderbolt. Ancient writers used the story to explain deserts and why some peoples had dark skin. Rubens shows the moment the horses scatter and the hours of the day flee in panic.

  • Hyperion → Helios, Selene, Eos
  • Phaeton loses the sun chariot
  • Etiological story of scorched lands
Hyperion
A Titan, father of the sun Helios, the moon Selene, and the dawn Eos.
Phaeton
Son of Helios who wrecked the sun’s chariot and was killed by Zeus’s thunderbolt.
