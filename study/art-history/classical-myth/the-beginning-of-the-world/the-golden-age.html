Lucas Cranach the Elder

The Golden Age

ca. 1530

Oil on panel · Nasjonalmuseet, Oslo

Made for: An oak panel picturing a mythical paradise; its original owner is not recorded.

Hesiod describes five races of humans, each worse than the last. In the Golden Age under Cronus, people lived like gods, without work, illness, or old age, and died as if falling asleep. Cranach paints them dancing and bathing in a walled garden, an image close to the Christian Eden. The silver race was foolish, the bronze violent, and the heroes better, but Hesiod’s own Iron Age is the worst of all.

  • Gold → Silver → Bronze → Heroes → Iron
  • Golden Age: no toil, no old age
  • Hesiod lives in the Iron Age
Five Ages (Races)
Hesiod’s history of humankind: Golden, Silver, Bronze, the Age of Heroes, and the present Iron Age.

“they lived like gods without sorrow of heart, remote and free from toil and grief”

Hesiod, Works and Days 112–113, trans. Evelyn-White
