The Beginning of the World · The Origins of Mortals

Dirck van Baburen

Prometheus Being Chained by Vulcan

1623

Oil on canvas · Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam

Made for: A dramatic gallery painting by a Utrecht follower of Caravaggio; no patron or first owner is recorded.

Vulcan, the smith, hammers chains onto Prometheus while Mercury points to the punishment. Baburen borrows Caravaggio’s strong light and dark and his plain, physical bodies, so the scene feels like a real execution. By this time Vulcan stood for technology itself, and Mercury for cunning, the same qualities that got Prometheus into trouble.

  • Vulcan chains, Mercury oversees
  • Caravaggesque light and realism
  • Technology punishing its giver

Themes

  • The trickster who made us human
