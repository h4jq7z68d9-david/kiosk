The Beginning of the World · Creation and the Rise of Zeus

Alexandre Cabanel

The Birth of Venus

1875

Oil on canvas · Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Made for: A smaller version of Cabanel’s famous 1863 Paris Salon painting, made for an American collector.

In Hesiod’s account, Aphrodite is born from the foam that gathered around Uranus’s severed genitals, which is how ancient writers explained her name (aphros means “foam”). Cabanel turns the story into a dreamy academic nude floating on the waves with cupids overhead. The original version was bought by Napoleon III, showing how mythology gave respectable cover to the nude in 19th-century art.

  • Born from sea foam (aphros)
