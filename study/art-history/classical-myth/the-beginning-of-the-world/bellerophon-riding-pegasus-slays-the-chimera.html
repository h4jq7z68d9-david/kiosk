The Beginning of the World · Creation and the Rise of Zeus

Peter Paul Rubens

Bellerophon, Riding Pegasus, Slays the Chimera

1635

Oil on panel · Musée Bonnat-Helleu, Bayonne

Made for: A small oil sketch, the kind Rubens painted to work out a design before his workshop made the full-size picture.

Gaea also mated with Pontus, the sea, and her descendants include a line of monsters. The Chimera breathed fire and had a lion’s body, a goat’s head rising from its back, and a snake for a tail. The hero Bellerophon killed it while riding the winged horse Pegasus, which he had tamed with a golden bridle from Athena. Rubens drives the hero down from the sky onto the monster in one diagonal sweep.

  • Lion, goat, and serpent in one body
  • Killed by Bellerophon on Pegasus
  • Oil sketch: a design worked out quickly
Pontus
The sea, born from Gaea alone; through his children she becomes ancestor of many monsters.
Chimera
Fire-breathing monster, part lion, part goat, part serpent, killed by Bellerophon.
Gorgons
Three snake-haired sisters, including Medusa, whose gaze turned people to stone.
Geryon
A three-bodied giant whose cattle Heracles stole, guarded by Orthus.
Orthus
Geryon’s two-headed guard dog.
Cerberus
The many-headed dog guarding the gate of the underworld.
Hydra
Many-headed water serpent that grew new heads when one was cut off, killed by Heracles.
