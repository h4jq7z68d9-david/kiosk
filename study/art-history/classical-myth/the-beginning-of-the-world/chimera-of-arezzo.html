Chimera of Arezzo

Etruscan, ca. 400 BCE

Bronze · National Archaeological Museum, Florence

Made for: An offering to the Etruscan sky god (an inscription on its leg reads tinscvil, “gift to Tinia”), discovered at Arezzo in 1553.

Gaea also mated with Pontus, the sea, and her descendants include a line of monsters. The Chimera breathed fire and had a lion’s body, a goat’s head rising from its back, and a snake for a tail; the hero Bellerophon killed it while riding the winged horse Pegasus. This bronze shows the wounded beast snarling, and the goat’s head is already bleeding.

  • Lion, goat, and serpent in one body
  • Killed by Bellerophon on Pegasus
  • Etruscan votive bronze
Pontus
The sea, born from Gaea alone; through his children she becomes ancestor of many monsters.
Chimera
Fire-breathing monster, part lion, part goat, part serpent, killed by Bellerophon.
Gorgons
Three snake-haired sisters, including Medusa, whose gaze turned people to stone.
Geryon
A three-bodied giant whose cattle Heracles stole, guarded by Orthus.
Orthus
Geryon’s two-headed guard dog.
Cerberus
The many-headed dog guarding the gate of the underworld.
Hydra
Many-headed water serpent that grew new heads when one was cut off, killed by Heracles.

  • Monsters with borrowed bodies
