The Flood Tablet (Epic of Gilgamesh, Tablet XI)

Neo-Assyrian, 7th century BCE

Clay · British Museum, London

Made for: A tablet from the library of the Assyrian king Ashurbanipal at Nineveh, a royal collection of literature and learning.

When George Smith of the British Museum translated this tablet in 1872, he found a flood story strikingly like Noah’s: the gods decide to destroy humanity, one god warns a good man to build a boat, and he releases birds to find land. Older Sumerian and Akkadian versions name the hero Ziusudra and Atrahasis. The Greek flood story belongs to the same family.

  • Translated by George Smith, 1872
  • Boat, warning god, birds sent out
  • Mesopotamian flood stories predate Genesis
Ziusudra and Enki
In the Sumerian flood story, the god Enki warns the good king Ziusudra to build a boat.
Atrahasis and Ea
The Akkadian version of the same story; Ea is the Akkadian name of Enki.
Noah and Yahweh
In Genesis, God warns Noah to build the ark before a flood sent to punish human wickedness.
