The Beginning of the World · The Origins of Mortals

Jean Cousin the Elder

Eva Prima Pandora

ca. 1550

Oil on panel · Louvre, Paris

Made for: The Louvre acquired it in 1922 from Sens, Cousin’s home town; the title, “Eve the first Pandora,” spells out its argument.

Cousin merges two first women: Eve of Genesis and Pandora of Greek myth. She reclines with a skull under her arm, an apple branch, and a jar, while a snake curls around her arm. Both stories blame a woman’s curiosity for bringing suffering into the world, and Renaissance thinkers liked to treat pagan myths as echoes of the Bible.

  • Eve and Pandora combined
  • Skull, apple, jar, snake
  • Both blame a woman for human suffering

Themes

  • Woman as a container
