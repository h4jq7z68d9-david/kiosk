Prometheus Carrying Fire

1636–38

Oil on canvas · Museo del Prado, Madrid

Made for: Painted for Philip IV’s hunting lodge, the Torre de la Parada, as part of Rubens’s workshop series on Ovid’s myths.

At a sacrifice at Mecone, Prometheus hid the good meat of an ox inside its unappetizing stomach and wrapped the bones in shining fat, and Zeus chose the fat. That trick explained why Greeks burned bones and fat for the gods and ate the meat themselves. Zeus retaliated by hiding fire, and Prometheus stole it back in a hollow fennel stalk, the burning torch he runs with here.

  • Trick at Mecone: bones in fat
  • Etiology of Greek sacrifice
  • Fire stolen in a fennel stalk

“stole again for men from Zeus the counsellor in a hollow fennel-stalk”
