Farnese Atlas

Roman, 2nd century CE, copy of an earlier original

Marble · National Archaeological Museum, Naples

Made for: A Roman statue that doubles as a sky map: the globe on Atlas’s shoulders is carved with the constellations.

Atlas, a Titan who fought against Zeus, was punished by being made to hold up the heavens at the western edge of the world. The sculptor shows him bent under a globe carved with the constellations, one of the oldest surviving images of the star groups. His name lives on in the word for a book of maps.

  • Titan punished after the Titanomachy
  • Holds up the sky
  • Globe carved with constellations
