Pergamon Altar: Athena fighting the Giant Alcyoneus

Hellenistic, ca. 180–160 BCE

Marble relief · Pergamon Museum, Berlin

Made for: Part of the frieze wrapping a monumental altar built by the kings of Pergamon, who compared their victories over the Gauls to the gods’ victory over the Giants.

The Gigantomachy was the last great battle, in which the Olympians, with the help of the mortal hero Heracles, defeated the Giants. On this frieze Athena grabs the winged Giant Alcyoneus by the hair, while his mother Gaea rises from the ground pleading for him and Victory flies in to crown the goddess. The writhing, deeply carved bodies are typical of Hellenistic drama.

  • Gigantomachy: gods vs. Giants
  • Gaea pleads for her son
  • Political message for Pergamon’s kings
Gigantomachy
The war of the Olympians against the Giants, often used in art as a symbol of order defeating barbarism.
