The Beginning of the World · Creation and the Rise of Zeus

Cornelis van Haarlem

The Fall of the Titans

1588–90

Oil on canvas · Statens Museum for Kunst, Copenhagen

Made for: A showpiece of Dutch Mannerism, made to display the painter’s skill with twisting, muscular nudes.

The Titanomachy was a ten-year war between the Titans and Zeus’s Olympians. Zeus won only after freeing the Cyclopes, who forged his thunderbolt, and the Hundred-Handers, who hurled boulders. The defeated Titans were locked in Tartarus, and Atlas was condemned to hold up the sky. Van Haarlem shows the Titans tumbling down in a tangle of bodies.

  • Titanomachy: ten-year war
  • Cyclopes and Hundred-Handers help Zeus
  • Losers imprisoned in Tartarus
Titanomachy
The war between the Titans and the Olympians that made Zeus king.
Cyclopes
Three one-eyed sons of Gaea and Uranus, smiths who forged Zeus’s thunderbolt.
Hecatonchires (Hundred-Handers)
Three giants with fifty heads and a hundred arms each who threw boulders at the Titans.
