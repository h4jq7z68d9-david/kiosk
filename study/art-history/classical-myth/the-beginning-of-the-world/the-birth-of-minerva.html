René-Antoine Houasse

The Birth of Minerva

1688

Oil on canvas · Palace of Versailles

Made for: One of a series on the story of Minerva that Houasse, a pupil of Charles Le Brun, painted for the royal palaces of Louis XIV.

Zeus swallowed his pregnant wife Metis, and later, suffering a terrible headache, had his head split open with an axe, usually by Hephaestus. Out sprang Athena, fully grown and armed. Houasse sets the scene on the clouds of Olympus, with the gods gathered as Hephaestus lowers his axe and the armored goddess flies up from her father’s head. The story makes her the child of Zeus alone, which is why she always takes his side.

  • Athena born from Zeus’s head
  • Hephaestus’s axe splits the skull
