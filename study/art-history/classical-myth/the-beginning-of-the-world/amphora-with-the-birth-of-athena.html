Amphora with the birth of Athena

Athenian, ca. 550–525 BCE

Black-figure terracotta · Louvre, Paris

Made for: A storage jar for wine or oil, painted with a popular myth for an Athenian buyer; Athena was the city’s goddess.

Zeus swallowed his pregnant wife Metis, and later, suffering a terrible headache, had his head split open with an axe, usually by Hephaestus. Out sprang Athena, fully grown and armed. The painter shows the tiny armored goddess emerging above the seated Zeus. Black-figure painters painted figures in black slip and scratched in details, leaving the red clay as background.

  • Athena born from Zeus’s head
  • Hephaestus’s axe splits the skull

Black-figure
