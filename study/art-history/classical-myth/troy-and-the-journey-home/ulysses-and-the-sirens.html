John William Waterhouse

Ulysses and the Sirens

1891

Oil on canvas · National Gallery of Victoria, Melbourne

Made for: A Royal Academy painting that deliberately revived the ancient bird-woman form of the Sirens, rather than the mermaids of later art.

Waterhouse copies the Greek vase type, filling the sky with bird-bodied Sirens swooping around the ship. Beyond them lay the narrow strait between Scylla, a six-headed monster who snatched six of the crew, and Charybdis, a whirlpool. On the island of Thrinacia the starving crew ate the sacred cattle of Helios, and Zeus destroyed the ship, leaving Odysseus alone.

  • Bird-Sirens circle the ship
  • Scylla and Charybdis
  • Cattle of Helios eaten; crew destroyed
Scylla and Charybdis
A six-headed monster and a whirlpool on either side of a narrow strait; “between Scylla and Charybdis” means between two dangers.
Cattle of Helios
Sacred cattle on Thrinacia; Eurylochus led the crew in eating them, and Zeus destroyed the ship.
