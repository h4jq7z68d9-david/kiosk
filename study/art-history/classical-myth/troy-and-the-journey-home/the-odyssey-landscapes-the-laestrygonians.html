The Odyssey Landscapes: the Laestrygonians

Roman, ca. 60–40 BCE

Wall painting from a house on the Esquiline Hill, Rome · Vatican Library

Made for: Part of a continuous painted frieze of Odysseus’s adventures in a Roman house, found in 1848.

The wind-king Aeolus gave Odysseus a bag holding all the adverse winds, but within sight of Ithaca his men opened it, hoping for treasure, and were blown back. Next the cannibal Laestrygonians destroyed every ship but Odysseus’s own. The painter sets the tiny figures of giants hurling rocks in a vast, airy landscape, one of the earliest surviving landscape paintings in Western art.
