Troy and the Journey Home · The Odyssey

J. M. W. Turner

Ulysses Deriding Polyphemus

1829

Oil on canvas · National Gallery, London

Made for: A Royal Academy exhibition painting, one of Turner’s grandest treatments of classical myth.

After sacking the Cicones in Thrace and escaping the Lotus-Eaters, whose fruit made men forget home, Odysseus and his men were trapped in the cave of the Cyclops Polyphemus, who ate them two at a time. Odysseus got him drunk, told him his name was “Nobody,” blinded him with a stake, and escaped under the bellies of his sheep. As he sailed off he shouted his real name, and Polyphemus’s father Poseidon cursed his voyage. Turner shows the ship sailing into a blazing sunrise while the giant writhes on the cliff.
