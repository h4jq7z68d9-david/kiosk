Laocoön and His Sons

ca. 1st century BCE–1st century CE

Marble · Vatican Museums · photograph ca. 1860–1900

Made for: Found in Rome in 1506, probably from the palace of the emperor Titus; Pope Julius II bought it within weeks, and it inspired Michelangelo.

To take Troy the Greeks needed Achilles’ son Neoptolemus, the bow of Heracles, and the stolen statue of Athena called the Palladium. Then they built a wooden horse, hid soldiers inside, and pretended to sail away. The Trojan priest Laocoön warned against it and threw a spear at it; two giant serpents came from the sea and crushed him and his sons. In Virgil, Trojans took this as a sign and dragged the horse inside.

  • Neoptolemus, Heracles’ bow, the Palladium
  • Laocoön warns against the horse
  • Serpents crush him and his sons
Laocoön
Trojan priest killed by sea serpents after warning against the wooden horse.
Neoptolemus
Achilles’ son, brought to Troy after his father’s death.
Palladium
The ancient statue of Athena that protected Troy, stolen by Odysseus and Diomedes.
