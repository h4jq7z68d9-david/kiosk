Siren Painter

Stamnos with Odysseus and the Sirens

Athenian, ca. 480–470 BCE

Red-figure terracotta · British Museum, London

Made for: A jar for mixing or storing wine, giving its name to the painter who decorated it.

After visiting the underworld, Odysseus sailed past the Sirens, whose song lured sailors to their deaths. He plugged his crew’s ears with wax and had himself tied to the mast so he could hear the song safely. The painter shows the Sirens as birds with women’s heads, one plunging toward the sea, perhaps in despair at her failure.

  • Sirens’ deadly song
  • Crew’s ears waxed; Odysseus tied to mast
  • Sirens as bird-women
