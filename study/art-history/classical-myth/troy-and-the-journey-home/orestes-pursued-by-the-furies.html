Troy and the Journey Home · The Trojan War

William-Adolphe Bouguereau

Orestes Pursued by the Furies

1862

Oil on canvas · Chrysler Museum of Art, Norfolk, Virginia

Made for: A large Salon painting, the kind of dramatic classical scene that made academic painters’ reputations.

The returns home, or nostoi, were often deadly. Agamemnon came back to Mycenae with Cassandra and was murdered in his bath by Clytemnestra and her lover Aegisthus. Years later his son Orestes, urged by his sister Electra and Apollo, killed his mother and Aegisthus. The Furies, avengers of family blood, hounded him until Athena’s court in Athens acquitted him. Bouguereau shows Orestes covering his ears as the Furies point at his dead mother.

  • Agamemnon murdered on his return
  • Orestes avenges him by killing his mother
  • Furies pursue him; Athens acquits him
