El Greco

Laocoön

ca. 1610–14

Oil on canvas · National Gallery of Art, Washington

Made for: El Greco’s only surviving painting of a classical myth, made late in his life in Toledo, Spain.

To take Troy the Greeks needed Achilles’ son Neoptolemus, the bow of Heracles, and the stolen statue of Athena called the Palladium. Then they built a wooden horse, hid soldiers inside, and pretended to sail away. The Trojan priest Laocoön warned against it; two giant serpents came from the sea and crushed him and his sons. El Greco sets the scene before a view of Toledo, with the horse heading for the city gate.

  • Neoptolemus, Heracles’ bow, the Palladium
  • Laocoön warns against the horse
  • Serpents crush him and his sons
Laocoön
Trojan priest killed by sea serpents after warning against the wooden horse.
