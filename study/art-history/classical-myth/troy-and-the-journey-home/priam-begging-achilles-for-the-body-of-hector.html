Troy and the Journey Home · The Trojan War

Alexander Ivanov

Priam Begging Achilles for the Body of Hector

1824

Oil on canvas · Tretyakov Gallery, Moscow

Made for: A student painting at the St. Petersburg Academy of Arts, which awarded it a gold medal.

In the Iliad’s final book, old King Priam, guided by Hermes, crosses the Greek camp at night to beg Achilles for his son’s body. He kisses the hands that killed Hector and asks Achilles to remember his own father. The two enemies weep together, and Achilles returns the body. The poem ends with Hector’s funeral, not with the fall of Troy.

  • Priam goes to Achilles by night
  • Shared grief between enemies
  • Iliad ends with Hector’s funeral
