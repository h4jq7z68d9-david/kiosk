Troy and the Journey Home · The Trojan War

Giovanni Domenico Tiepolo

The Procession of the Trojan Horse into Troy

ca. 1760

Oil on canvas · National Gallery, London

Made for: A small painting, probably a study for a larger work, by the son of Giambattista Tiepolo.

The Trojans pulled the horse into the city and celebrated. That night the Greeks climbed out, opened the gates, and Troy was sacked. Priam was killed at an altar, Astyanax thrown from the walls, Polyxena sacrificed at Achilles’ tomb, and Cassandra, Apollo’s disbelieved prophetess, dragged from Athena’s shrine. Euripides’ Trojan Women shows the captured queens waiting to be shipped away as slaves.
