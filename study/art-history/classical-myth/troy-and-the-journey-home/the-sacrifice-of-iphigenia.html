The Sacrifice of Iphigenia

Roman, 1st century CE

Wall painting from the House of the Tragic Poet, Pompeii · National Archaeological Museum, Naples

Made for: Part of the decoration of a Pompeian house named for its many scenes of drama and poetry.

The Greek fleet gathered at Aulis, but Artemis held back the winds. The seer Calchas declared that Agamemnon, the commander, must sacrifice his daughter Iphigenia. In the painting she is carried to the altar while Calchas raises a knife and Agamemnon, at left, covers his face; above, Artemis appears with the deer that, in some versions, replaced the girl at the last moment. Clytemnestra never forgave her husband.

  • Fleet stalled at Aulis
  • Agamemnon sacrifices Iphigenia
  • A deer substituted in some versions
Iphigenia
Daughter of Agamemnon and Clytemnestra, sacrificed (or rescued by Artemis) at Aulis.
Calchas
The Greek army’s seer.
Palamedes
Clever Greek hero who exposed Odysseus’s feigned madness and was later framed by him.
