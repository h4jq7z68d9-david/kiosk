Troy and the Journey Home · The Odyssey

John William Waterhouse

Circe Offering the Cup to Ulysses

1891

Oil on canvas · Gallery Oldham, England

Made for: A Royal Academy painting; Waterhouse returned to enchantresses from myth throughout his career.

On the island of Aeaea the witch Circe turned Odysseus’s men into pigs. Hermes gave Odysseus a magic herb, moly, that protected him, and he forced Circe to restore his men; he then stayed with her for a year. Waterhouse puts the viewer in Odysseus’s place: Circe holds out the cup and wand, and Odysseus appears only as a reflection in the mirror behind her throne.

  • Circe turns men into pigs
  • Hermes’s herb, moly, protects Odysseus
  • Viewer stands in Odysseus’s place
Circe
Witch-goddess of Aeaea, daughter of Helios.
Moly
Magic herb from Hermes that protected Odysseus from Circe’s drugs.
