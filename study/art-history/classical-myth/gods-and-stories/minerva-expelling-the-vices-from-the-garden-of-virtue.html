Minerva Expelling the Vices from the Garden of Virtue

completed 1502

Tempera on canvas, 160 × 192 cm · Louvre, Paris

Made for Isabella d’Este’s studiolo in Mantua, a small private study she filled with paintings on moral themes.

  • One of five canvases for the room; it hung opposite his Parnassus.
  • Look for the Vices in flight, among them armless Sloth and Ignorance carried by Ingratitude.
  • Hidden images: a tree in human form and a face in a cloud.
  • Inscriptions in several alphabets label the figures.
  • Later owned by Cardinal Richelieu; entered the French state collections in 1801.

Minerva was a natural champion of virtue over vice. Here the myth is used as a moral lesson rather than a story.
