Rembrandt

The Abduction of Proserpina

1631

Oil on oak panel, 84.4 × 79.5 cm · Gemäldegalerie, Berlin

One of the artist’s small-figure history paintings from around the time he moved from Leiden to Amsterdam, and a rare subject in Dutch art. It was probably in the collection of the Dutch stadholder Frederik Hendrik by 1632.

  • Proserpina screams and claws at Pluto’s face while her companions grab at her cloak.
  • Bright blue sky at upper left (the earth) gives way to darkness at lower right (Pluto’s realm).
  • The blue is costly ultramarine, made from lapis lazuli, and is original.
  • Technical study shows he first painted four horses, later reduced to two.
  • The design likely depends on a composition by Rubens known from an engraving.

Pluto seized Proserpina, daughter of the harvest goddess Ceres, while she gathered flowers, and carried her down to rule as his queen.
