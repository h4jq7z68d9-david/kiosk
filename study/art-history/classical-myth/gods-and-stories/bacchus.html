Caravaggio

Bacchus

c. 1598

Oil on canvas, 95 × 85 cm · Uffizi Gallery, Florence

An early work: a young model in a white sheet posing as the wine god, offering a glass. Cardinal del Monte, the painter’s first patron, gave it to the Medici for the wedding of Cosimo II in 1608.

  • Rediscovered in the Uffizi storerooms in 1913 and attributed to the painter by Roberto Longhi.
  • The fruit bowl includes a half-rotten apple among pomegranates, quinces, figs and grapes.
  • Look closely at the carafe: a tiny reflected face, generally read as a self-portrait, was confirmed by infrared study.
  • Grapes and vine leaves sit in the dark curls; the fingernails are dirty.

Dionysus (Roman Bacchus), son of Zeus and the mortal Semele, is god of wine, release and theatre.
