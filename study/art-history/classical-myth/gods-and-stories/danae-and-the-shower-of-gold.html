Danaë and the Shower of Gold

1560–65

Oil on canvas, 129.8 × 181.2 cm · Museo del Prado, Madrid

A new, horizontal version of the Danaë the painter had sent Philip II in 1553, painted with looser handling. The museum’s catalogue thinks it was made for a private patron who knew him, not for the king.

  • The old nurse catching the gold in her apron sets youth against age, nude against clothed.
  • Gold pours from the cloud like falling coins.
  • A small dog on the bed is read by the museum as a hint of lust.
  • Kept from 1827 in a restricted room of Madrid’s Academy of San Fernando.

King Acrisius locked his daughter Danaë away after an oracle said her son would kill him. Zeus reached her as a shower of gold, and she bore the hero Perseus.
