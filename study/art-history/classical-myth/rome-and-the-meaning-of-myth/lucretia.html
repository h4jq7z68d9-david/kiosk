Rembrandt

Lucretia

1664

Oil on canvas · National Gallery of Art, Washington

Made for: A late painting focused on a single, tragic figure; its first owner is not recorded.

Sextus Tarquinius, son of Rome’s last king, raped the virtuous Lucretia, wife of Tarquinius Collatinus. She told her husband and father what had happened and stabbed herself. Lucius Junius Brutus swore on her blood to drive out the kings, and in 509 BCE the Romans founded the Republic. Rembrandt shows her in the moment before the blow, dagger raised, face full of sorrow.

  • Lucretia’s rape and suicide
  • Brutus’s oath
  • Kings expelled; Republic founded
