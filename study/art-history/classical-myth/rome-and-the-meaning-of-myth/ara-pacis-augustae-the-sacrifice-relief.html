Ara Pacis Augustae: the sacrifice relief

13–9 BCE

Marble · Museum of the Ara Pacis, Rome · historical photograph

Made for: A panel of the Altar of Augustan Peace, voted by the Senate to celebrate Augustus’s return from Spain and Gaul.

In Italy Aeneas, guided by the Cumaean Sibyl, visited the underworld, passing the Mourning Fields of tragic lovers and reaching Elysium, where Anchises showed him the future heroes of Rome. Landing in Latium, he found the sign of a white sow, allied with King Evander, and won Lavinia, daughter of King Latinus, by defeating the Rutulian prince Turnus. This relief shows a veiled man sacrificing a sow, traditionally read as Aeneas, though some scholars argue it is Rome’s second king, Numa.
