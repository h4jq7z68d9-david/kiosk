Guercino

The Death of Dido

1631

Oil on canvas · Galleria Spada, Rome

Made for: Commissioned by Cardinal Bernardino Spada as a gift for Maria de’ Medici, queen of France; when that fell through he bought it himself in 1631, and it remains in his family’s palace collection.

Jupiter sent Mercury to remind Aeneas that his destiny lay in Italy, and he left Carthage in secret. Dido built a pyre, cursed the Trojans, and stabbed herself with his sword. Her curse explained the later Punic Wars between Rome and Carthage. Guercino shows her collapsed on the pyre as her sister and attendants rush to her, while Aeneas’s ships sail away in the distance.

  • Aeneas leaves at Jupiter’s command
  • Dido’s curse and suicide
  • Origin story of the Punic Wars
Punic Wars
Three wars (264–146 BCE) between Rome and Carthage, which Dido’s curse was said to foreshadow.
