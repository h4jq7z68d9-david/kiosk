Ovide moralisé en prose

15th century

Illuminated manuscript · Bibliothèque nationale de France, Paris

Made for: A French prose version of a moralized Ovid, made for an aristocratic reader who wanted the stories with their Christian meaning.

Medieval Christians kept reading Ovid by treating his myths as allegories of Christian truth. The long French poem called the Ovide moralisé (about 1300) retells the Metamorphoses and then explains each story: Phaethon’s fall stands for pride, Orpheus can stand for Christ. Prose versions like this one carried the method into the 1400s, and it shaped how Renaissance artists understood classical subjects.

  • Ovid retold with Christian morals
  • About 1300; prose versions later
  • Kept myth alive in the Middle Ages
