Rome and the Meaning of Myth · Aeneas and the Founding of Rome

Federico Barocci

Aeneas Fleeing Troy

1598

Oil on canvas · Galleria Borghese, Rome

Made for: A second version of a painting Barocci first made for the emperor Rudolf II; the young Bernini knew it, and it shaped his own marble group of the subject.

Aeneas, son of Venus and the Trojan Anchises, escapes burning Troy carrying his old father, who holds the household gods, and leading his small son Ascanius, also called Iulus. Virgil wrote the Aeneid under Augustus, whose family, the Julii, claimed descent from Iulus. Barocci sets the three generations against the flames, with Aeneas’s wife Creusa following behind; she will be lost in the escape. The group is the image of Roman pietas.

  • Aeneas carries Anchises, leads Ascanius
  • Iulus: ancestor of the Julian family
  • Three generations = pietas
