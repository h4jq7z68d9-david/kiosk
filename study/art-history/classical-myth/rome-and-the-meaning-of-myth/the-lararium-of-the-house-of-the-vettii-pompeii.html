The Lararium of the House of the Vettii, Pompeii

1895

Watercolor of a household shrine of ca. 62–79 CE

Made for: A record painted soon after excavation; the shrine itself was where the household offered daily worship to its protective spirits.

Roman religion centered on the home. In this shrine the household Lares dance on either side of the Genius, the spirit of the head of the family, while a serpent below protects the house. Families offered food, wine, and incense here daily. Romans renamed Greek gods for their own: Asclepius became Aesculapius, Persephone Proserpina, Odysseus Ulysses, and Hades Dis or Pluto.

Lararium
A household shrine for the Lares.
Aesculapius
The Roman name for Asclepius, god of medicine, brought to Rome in 291 BCE.
