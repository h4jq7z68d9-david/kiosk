Augustus of Prima Porta

Early 1st century CE, after a bronze of ca. 20 BCE

Marble · Vatican Museums · photograph ca. 1875–1900

Made for: A statue found in 1863 at the villa of Augustus’s wife Livia, presenting the emperor as a commander and a near-divine leader.

Augustus stands like a Greek statue, addressing his troops, his breastplate showing the return of Roman standards from Parthia. At his feet a small Cupid rides a dolphin, a reminder that the Julian family descended from Venus through Aeneas. The statue puts Virgil’s myth into stone: the emperor as the destined heir of Troy.

  • Emperor as general and orator
  • Cupid on a dolphin: descent from Venus
  • Myth used as imperial propaganda
