Capitoline Wolf

Date debated (traditionally 5th century BCE; twins added ca. 1471–1509)

Bronze · Capitoline Museums, Rome

Made for: The symbol of Rome; the twins were added in the Renaissance when the wolf was given to the people of Rome.

In Livy’s history, King Numitor of Alba Longa was overthrown by his brother Amulius, who forced Numitor’s daughter Rhea Silvia to become a Vestal Virgin. Mars fathered twins with her anyway, and Amulius had them thrown into the Tiber. A she-wolf nursed them until the shepherd Faustulus and his wife Larentia raised them; lupa, “wolf,” was also slang for a prostitute, which may explain the story. The wolf’s date is now disputed, with some tests suggesting it was cast in the Middle Ages.
