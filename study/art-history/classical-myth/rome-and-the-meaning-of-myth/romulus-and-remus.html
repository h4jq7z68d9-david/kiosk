Peter Paul Rubens

Romulus and Remus

ca. 1615–16

Oil on canvas · Capitoline Museums, Rome

Made for: Its first owner is not recorded; it is now in the museum on the Capitoline Hill, the heart of ancient Rome.

Rubens paints the she-wolf licking one twin while the river god Tiber reclines nearby and the shepherd Faustulus arrives. Grown up, the twins restored their grandfather and founded a new city, but in a quarrel Romulus killed Remus and named Rome for himself. After his death he was said to have been taken up to heaven and worshipped as the god Quirinus.

  • She-wolf, Tiber, and Faustulus
  • Romulus kills Remus, founds Rome
  • Romulus deified as Quirinus
Quirinus
The god Romulus became after his death.
Apotheosis
Deification; Roman emperors were declared divus (divine) after death.
Triumph
A victory parade through Rome in which the triumphant general dressed as Jupiter.
