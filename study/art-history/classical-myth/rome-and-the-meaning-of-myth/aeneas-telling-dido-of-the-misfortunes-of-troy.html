Pierre-Narcisse Guérin

Aeneas Telling Dido of the Misfortunes of Troy

1815

Oil on canvas · Louvre, Paris

Made for: A Neoclassical history painting, staged like a scene from a play, that was a great success at the Paris Salon of 1817.

Aeneas wandered for years: to Thrace, where a bleeding bush warned him away; past the Harpies; to Epirus, where the seer Helenus guided him; and to Sicily, where he saw the blinded Polyphemus. Storms drove him to Carthage, newly founded by Queen Dido. As he told her the story of Troy, she fell in love with him, encouraged by Venus and Cupid, shown here as Ascanius on her lap.

  • Arrival at Carthage
