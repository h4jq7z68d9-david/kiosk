Rome and the Meaning of Myth · Interpreting Myth

Sandro Botticelli

Primavera

ca. 1480

Tempera on panel · Uffizi Gallery, Florence

Made for: Made for the Medici family, probably for a room in the Florence palace of Lorenzo di Pierfrancesco de’ Medici, possibly for a wedding.

Venus stands in her garden with Cupid above, flanked by the Three Graces, Mercury, Flora, and the west wind Zephyr seizing the nymph Chloris. Many scholars read it through the Neoplatonism of the Florentine philosopher Marsilio Ficino, who taught that earthly beauty and love lead the soul upward toward the divine. Whatever its exact meaning, it shows how Renaissance thinkers treated myth as philosophy.

  • Venus, Graces, Mercury, Flora, Zephyr
  • Neoplatonic reading: love leads upward
  • Myth as philosophy in Medici Florence
Neoplatonism
A philosophy, revived in Renaissance Florence, that saw earthly beauty as a reflection of divine beauty.
