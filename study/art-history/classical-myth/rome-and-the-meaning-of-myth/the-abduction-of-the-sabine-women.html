Nicolas Poussin

The Abduction of the Sabine Women

ca. 1633–34

Oil on canvas · Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Made for: Owned in the 1630s by the French ambassador to Rome and later by Cardinal Richelieu; it is staged like an ancient relief among classical buildings.

Rome had too few women, so Romulus invited the neighboring Sabines to a festival and, at his signal, the Romans seized their daughters. Later the Sabine king Tatius attacked, helped by the traitor Tarpeia, who opened the citadel and was crushed under the Sabines’ shields. The Sabine women, led by Romulus’s wife Hersilia, ran between the armies and made peace, and the two peoples merged. Poussin shows Romulus giving the signal from the steps at left.
