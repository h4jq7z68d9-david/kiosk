Bust of Pericles

Roman copy of a Greek original of ca. 430 BCE

Marble · Vatican Museums (Museo Chiaramonti)

Made for: A Roman copy of the honorary portrait set up on the Athenian Acropolis.

Pericles led Athens at its height in the mid-400s BCE, when the Parthenon was built and drama, history, and philosophy flourished. He wears a general’s helmet, pushed up to show his face. Athens was a democracy, but only free adult male citizens could vote, and the city’s wealth rested partly on slaves and on an empire of allied cities.

  • Leader of Classical Athens
  • Helmet = office of general
  • Democracy limited to male citizens
Polis (pl. poleis)
An independent Greek city-state with its own laws, army, and gods, such as Athens or Corinth.
Demos
The citizen body of a polis: free men who owned property, spoke and voted in the assembly, and fought in its wars.
Tyrants
Rulers who took power outside the old aristocracy (ca. 650–500 BCE), often with commercial backing; the bad sense of the word comes from aristocratic writers.
