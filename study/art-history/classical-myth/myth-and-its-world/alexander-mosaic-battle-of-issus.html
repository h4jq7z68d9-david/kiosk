Alexander Mosaic (Battle of Issus)

Roman, ca. 100 BCE, after a Greek painting of ca. 315 BCE

Floor mosaic from the House of the Faun, Pompeii · National Archaeological Museum, Naples

Made for: A floor mosaic of more than a million tiny stones made for one of the grandest houses in Pompeii, copying a lost Greek painting.

Alexander III of Macedon (356–323 BCE) conquered the Persian Empire and carried Greek culture to Egypt and India. At left he charges bareheaded on his horse Bucephalus, while the Persian king Darius turns in his chariot to flee. Alexander’s death in 323 BCE begins the Hellenistic period, when Greek kingdoms spread Greek art and myth across the eastern Mediterranean.

  • Conquered Persia, 334–323 BCE
  • Alexander vs. Darius at Issus (333 BCE)
  • Start of the Hellenistic period
