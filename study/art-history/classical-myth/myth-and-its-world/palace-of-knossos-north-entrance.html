Palace of Knossos, North Entrance

Minoan, ca. 1700–1450 BCE

Knossos, Crete · columns and bull fresco reconstructed by Arthur Evans

Made for: The center of a Bronze Age state: royal residence, storehouse, workshop, and ritual space, built without defensive walls.

The Minoans of Crete, named by the archaeologist Arthur Evans after the mythical King Minos, built huge palace complexes with no fortification walls. The maze of rooms at Knossos may lie behind the myth of the Labyrinth, and the bull images that decorated it echo the story of the Minotaur. The Minoans wrote in a script called Linear A that is still undeciphered.

  • Minoan Crete: palaces without walls
  • Possible source of Minos, Labyrinth, Minotaur myths
