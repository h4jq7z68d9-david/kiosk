Funerary krater

Late Geometric, ca. 750–735 BCE

Terracotta · Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Made for: A grave marker for an Athenian aristocrat: liquid offerings could be poured through its base, and its scenes show the funeral itself.

In the Geometric period Greek potters covered vases with bands of zigzags and meanders, and then added stick-like human figures. This huge krater shows the dead man laid out on a bier surrounded by mourners, with a procession of chariots and warriors below. Scenes like these appear just as the Greek alphabet was being adopted, around the time Homer’s poems took shape.

  • Geometric style: bands of pattern, stick figures
  • Funeral scene and chariot procession
  • Era of the new Greek alphabet
