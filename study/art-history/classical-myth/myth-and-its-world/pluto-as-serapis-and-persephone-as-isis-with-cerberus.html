Pluto (as Serapis) and Persephone (as Isis) with Cerberus

Roman, 2nd century CE

Marble · Heraklion Archaeological Museum, Crete

Made for: Cult statues from a sanctuary of Egyptian gods at Gortyn on Crete, where Romans worshipped the rulers of the underworld in Egyptian form.

Hades, the Roman Pluto (“the rich one”), rules the underworld and the dead. He is the oldest brother of Zeus and Poseidon but lives below the earth, so he is usually not counted among the Olympians. Here he stands with a scepter beside his three-headed dog Cerberus, next to his queen Persephone. The pair are dressed as the Egyptian gods Serapis and Isis, showing how Roman religion blended Greek and Egyptian gods.

  • God of the underworld and the dead
  • Attributes: scepter, Cerberus, helmet of invisibility
  • Not an Olympian: lives below
