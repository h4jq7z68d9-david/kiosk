Peter Paul Rubens and Frans Snyders

Ceres and Two Nymphs

ca. 1620–28

Oil on canvas · Museo del Prado, Madrid

Made for: A workshop collaboration: Rubens painted the figures and his Antwerp colleague Frans Snyders, a specialist in still life, painted the fruit and the cornucopia.

Demeter, the Roman Ceres, is the goddess of grain, farming, and the seasons; the word “cereal” comes from her Latin name. Rubens shows her as a calm, full-bodied goddess of plenty, standing with two nymphs who hold a horn of plenty overflowing with fruit. Her daughter Persephone’s yearly return from the underworld brings back the growing season, and Demeter’s rites at Eleusis offered worshippers hope for a better afterlife.

  • Goddess of grain and harvest
  • Attributes: sheaf of wheat, torch, cornucopia
  • Mother of Persephone; center of Eleusinian Mysteries
