Marble statue of a kouros (youth)

Archaic, ca. 590–580 BCE

Marble · Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Made for: A grave marker for a young Athenian man, showing him as an ideal youth rather than a portrait.

Kouroi (singular kouros) are standing nude youths, left foot forward and arms at their sides, a pose borrowed from Egyptian sculpture. This is one of the earliest surviving Athenian marble statues. Over the next century Greek sculptors gradually loosened this stiff pose into the natural, balanced bodies of the Classical period, a change that happened unusually fast.

  • Egyptian-inspired pose, left foot forward
  • Ideal youth, not a portrait
  • Start of fast change toward Classical naturalism
