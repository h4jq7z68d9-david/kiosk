Myth and Its World · The Cultural Context

“Treasury of Atreus”

Mycenaean, ca. 1250 BCE

Stone tholos tomb · Mycenae, Greece

Made for: A royal tomb: a long open passage (dromos) leads to a domed, beehive-shaped burial chamber (tholos).

The Mycenaeans were the Greek-speaking Bronze Age culture of the mainland, ruling from walled citadels such as Mycenae and Tiryns. Heinrich Schliemann, who excavated here in the 1870s, believed he had found the world of Homer’s king Agamemnon, and this tomb still carries the name of Agamemnon’s father. The Mycenaeans kept palace accounts in Linear B, an early form of Greek, but did not use writing for stories.

  • Mycenaean Greece: fortified citadels
  • Linked by tradition to Agamemnon’s family
  • Linear B used for records, not myth
