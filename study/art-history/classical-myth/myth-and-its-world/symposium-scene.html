Brygos Painter

Symposium scene

Athenian, ca. 490–480 BCE

Red-figure cup (interior) · British Museum, London

Made for: A drinking cup used at a symposium; the picture inside was revealed as the drinker emptied it.

A reclining young man hands a pair of pipes to a female musician. The symposium was an all-male evening of drinking, poetry, song, and conversation, and it was one of the main places where myths were retold and where painted cups showing them were used. The women present were paid entertainers, not wives.

Symposium
An all-male drinking party with poetry, music, and talk; a key setting for retelling myths.
Hetairai
Educated female companions paid to entertain men with music and conversation at symposia.
Aoidoi, rhapsodes
Aoidoi were singer-poets who composed myths in performance; rhapsodes later recited memorized epic poetry.
Pedagogue
The enslaved attendant who supervised a boy’s schooling, roughly from age 6 to 13.
Pederasty
A socially recognized relationship between an adult citizen man and an adolescent boy, framed as mentorship, common in aristocratic Athens.
