Attributed to Praxiteles

Hermes and the Infant Dionysus

ca. 340–330 BCE (some scholars date it later)

Marble · Archaeological Museum of Olympia · shown in a 19th-century photograph

Made for: A statue dedicated in the Temple of Hera at Olympia, where the traveler Pausanias saw it in the 2nd century CE; excavators found it there in 1877.

Hermes, the Roman Mercury, is the messenger of the gods, the god of travelers, trade, and thieves, and the guide who leads souls to the underworld. He is the son of Zeus and the nymph Maia. Here he carries his baby half-brother Dionysus to the nymphs who will raise him, safe from Hera’s anger, and once dangled a bunch of grapes in his missing right hand. The relaxed S-curve pose and soft, polished surfaces made Praxiteles the most admired sculptor of the 4th century BCE.

  • Messenger; travelers, trade, thieves
  • Attributes: caduceus, petasos (traveler’s hat), winged sandals
  • Leads souls to Hades; protects the infant Dionysus
