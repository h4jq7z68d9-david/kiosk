Apollo Belvedere

Roman copy, ca. 120–140 CE, after a Greek bronze of ca. 330 BCE

Marble · Vatican Museums

Made for: A Roman copy of a famous Greek statue; rediscovered in the late 1400s, it became a model of ideal male beauty for Renaissance and later artists.

Apollo, whose name stayed the same in Latin, is the god of light, music, poetry, prophecy, healing, and archery. He is the son of Zeus and Leto and the twin of Artemis. His oracle at Delphi was the most important in Greece. The statue shows him having just shot an arrow, his bow once in the outstretched left hand.

  • Light, music, prophecy, archery
  • Attributes: bow, lyre, laurel
  • Oracle at Delphi; twin of Artemis
