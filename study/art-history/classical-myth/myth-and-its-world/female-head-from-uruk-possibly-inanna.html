Female head from Uruk (possibly Inanna)

Sumerian, ca. 3200–3000 BCE

Marble · Iraq Museum, Baghdad

Made for: Probably the face of a cult statue from the temple precinct of Inanna at Uruk; the eyes and brows once held colored inlays.

Each Sumerian city had its own protecting god, who lived in a temple at the city’s center. Inanna, the “Queen of Heaven,” was the goddess of love and war, and Greek writers’ Aphrodite shares some of her traits. Many of the patterns of Greek myth, such as generations of gods and a great flood, appear first in Mesopotamia.
