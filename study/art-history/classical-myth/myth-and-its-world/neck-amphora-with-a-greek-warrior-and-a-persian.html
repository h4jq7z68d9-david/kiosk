Myth and Its World · The Cultural Context

Neck-amphora with a Greek warrior and a Persian

Athenian, early 5th century BCE

Red-figure terracotta · Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Made for: A storage jar for wine or oil; scenes of Greeks defeating Persians were popular after the Persian Wars.

A Greek hoplite with a round shield and spear attacks a Persian archer in trousers. Between 490 and 479 BCE the Greek city-states defeated two Persian invasions at battles including Marathon, Thermopylae, and Salamis. The victory gave Greeks a shared identity defined against “barbarian” Asia, an idea that runs through Classical art and its use of myth.

  • Persian Wars, 490–479 BCE
  • Greek vs. Persian = civilization vs. “other”
  • Hoplite armor: helmet, round shield, spear
Hoplite
A heavily armed Greek citizen-soldier who fought shoulder to shoulder in a phalanx, replacing the one-on-one duels of Homeric heroes.
Hellenes
The Greeks’ own name for themselves, from Hellas; “Greek” comes from the Romans.
