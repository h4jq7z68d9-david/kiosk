Victory Stele of Naram-Sin

Akkadian, ca. 2254–2218 BCE

Sandstone · Louvre, Paris

Made for: A public monument celebrating a royal victory; it was carried off to Susa as war booty more than a thousand years later.

The Akkadian king Naram-Sin climbs a mountain over defeated enemies, wearing the horned crown that marks a god. When the Semitic-speaking Akkadians conquered Sumer around 2300 BCE, they took over the Sumerian gods under new names and passed them to the Babylonians. That chain of borrowing is how Near Eastern stories reached the Greeks.
