Myth and Its World · The Nature of Myth

Peter Paul Rubens

Juno and Argus

ca. 1610–11

Oil on canvas · Wallraf-Richartz-Museum, Cologne

Made for: A large showpiece painted in Antwerp soon after Rubens returned from eight years in Italy, where he had studied ancient sculpture and Venetian color.

Hera, the Roman Juno, is queen of the gods and goddess of marriage and the family. She is Zeus’s sister and wife, and in myth she punishes his lovers and their children, most famously Heracles, whose name means “glory of Hera.” Here she and her messenger Iris take the hundred eyes from the severed head of her watchman Argus to set in the tails of her peacocks, the bird that became her emblem. His body lies in the foreground.

  • Queen of the gods; marriage and childbirth
  • Attributes: diadem, scepter, peacock, pomegranate
  • Pursues Zeus’s lovers and children
