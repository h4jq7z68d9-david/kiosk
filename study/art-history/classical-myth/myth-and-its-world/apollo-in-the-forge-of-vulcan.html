Myth and Its World · The Nature of Myth

Diego Velázquez

Apollo in the Forge of Vulcan

1630

Oil on canvas · Museo del Prado, Madrid

Made for: Painted during Velázquez’s first stay in Italy and later acquired for the Spanish royal collection.

Hephaestus, the Roman Vulcan, is the smith and craftsman of the gods, the maker of their weapons and palaces, and the only Olympian with a physical disability. Velázquez shows Apollo arriving in his forge to tell him that his wife, Aphrodite, is having an affair with Ares, and the workers freeze in shock. The word “volcano” comes from Vulcan, whose forge was imagined beneath the earth.

  • Fire, forge, metalwork
  • Attributes: hammer, anvil, tongs
  • Husband of Aphrodite
