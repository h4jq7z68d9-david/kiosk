Myth and Its World · The Cultural Context

Jacques-Louis David

Leonidas at Thermopylae

1814

Oil on canvas · Louvre, Paris

Made for: A large history painting meant to show patriotic self-sacrifice, finished in the last months of Napoleon’s empire.

In 480 BCE King Leonidas of Sparta and a small force held the narrow pass of Thermopylae against the Persian army before being killed. David shows Leonidas calmly seated among his men just before the final battle. The painting shows how later Europe treated Greek history itself almost as myth, a story of heroic virtue.

  • Thermopylae, 480 BCE
  • Spartan sacrifice as heroic model
  • Greek history used as moral example
