Hestia Giustiniani

Roman copy after a Greek original of ca. 470–460 BCE

Marble · Torlonia Collection, Rome · photographed on loan to the Louvre, 2024

Made for: A Roman copy of an Early Classical statue of a standing goddess; the identification as Hestia is traditional rather than certain.

Hestia, the Roman Vesta, is the goddess of the hearth, the fire at the center of every home and city. She has almost no stories of her own, because her role was simply to be present wherever a fire was kept. In Rome the Vestal Virgins tended the city’s sacred flame. Some later lists of the Twelve Olympians drop Hestia and include Dionysus instead.

  • Goddess of the hearth and household order
  • Sacred flame; Vestal Virgins in Rome
  • Sometimes replaced by Dionysus among the Twelve
Twelve Olympians
The main gods who live on Mount Olympus; the lists vary slightly, and Hades, who lives below, is usually left out.
