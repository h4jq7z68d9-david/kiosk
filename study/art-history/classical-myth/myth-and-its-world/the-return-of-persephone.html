Myth and Its World · The Nature of Myth

Frederic Leighton

The Return of Persephone

1891

Oil on canvas · Leeds Art Gallery

Made for: An exhibition painting by the president of the Royal Academy, made for a Victorian audience that loved classical subjects.

Persephone, the Roman Proserpina, is Demeter’s daughter and became queen of the underworld after Hades carried her off. Because she had eaten pomegranate seeds there, she must return to him for part of each year, and her absence explains winter. Leighton paints the moment Hermes lifts her out of the dark toward her mother, who reaches up in the sunlight. The story turns a family’s grief into the cycle of the seasons.

  • Queen of the underworld; goddess of spring
  • Pomegranate seeds bind her to Hades
  • Her yearly return explains the seasons
