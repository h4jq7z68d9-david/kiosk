Jupiter of Smyrna

Roman, 2nd century CE

Marble · Louvre, Paris

Made for: A Roman statue of the chief god, found at Smyrna (modern İzmir) in 1680 and sent to Louis XIV; the arm with the thunderbolt and the eagle are later restorations.

Zeus, called Jupiter by the Romans, is the king of the gods and the god of the sky and thunder. He is the youngest son of the Titans Cronus and Rhea and became ruler after overthrowing his father. As judge of oaths and guest-friendship he stands for order and justice, yet the myths also give him more lovers than any other god, which explains the many demigod heroes. The sculptor shows him the standard way: a mature bearded man, bare-chested, with a scepter and thunderbolt.

  • King of the gods; sky, weather, thunder
  • Attributes: thunderbolt, eagle, scepter, oak
  • Brother-husband of Hera
