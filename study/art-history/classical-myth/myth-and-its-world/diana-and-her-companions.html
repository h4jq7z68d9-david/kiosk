Johannes Vermeer

Diana and Her Companions

ca. 1653–56

Oil on canvas · Mauritshuis, The Hague

Made for: One of Vermeer’s earliest surviving works, painted when he still hoped to be a history painter, before he turned to the quiet interiors he is known for.

Artemis, the Roman Diana, is the virgin goddess of the hunt, wild animals, the moon, and childbirth. Twin sister of Apollo, she lives in the wilderness among nymphs. Vermeer shows her without her bow, resting at dusk while a nymph washes her feet; only the small crescent moon on her brow and the hunting dog identify her. Stories warn of what happens to men who see her bathing, like the hunter Actaeon.

  • Hunt, wilderness, moon, childbirth
  • Attributes: bow and quiver, crescent moon, deer, hunting dog
  • Protector of girls before marriage
