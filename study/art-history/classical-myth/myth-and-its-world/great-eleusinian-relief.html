Great Eleusinian Relief

Greek, ca. 440–430 BCE

Marble · National Archaeological Museum, Athens

Made for: A votive relief from the sanctuary of Demeter at Eleusis, the site of the secret rites known as the Eleusinian Mysteries.

Demeter, the Roman Ceres, is the goddess of grain, farming, and the seasons; the word “cereal” comes from her Latin name. Here she hands ears of grain to the young prince Triptolemus, who will teach farming to humankind, while her daughter Persephone, holding a torch, crowns him. The relief stood at Eleusis, where worshippers were initiated into rites believed to give hope for a better afterlife.

  • Goddess of grain and harvest
  • Attributes: sheaf of wheat, torch, poppy
  • Mother of Persephone; center of Eleusinian Mysteries
