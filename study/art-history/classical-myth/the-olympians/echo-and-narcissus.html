John William Waterhouse

Echo and Narcissus

1903

Oil on canvas · Walker Art Gallery, Liverpool

Made for: A Royal Academy exhibition painting drawn from Ovid’s Metamorphoses.

Hermes’s son Pan, half man and half goat, is the god of shepherds and wild places; sudden fear in lonely country was blamed on him, giving us the word “panic.” In one story Pan pursued the nymph Echo, who rejected him and was torn apart until only her voice remained. In Ovid’s better-known version, shown here, Echo pines for Narcissus, who falls in love with his own reflection.

  • Pan: goat-legged god of the wild
  • Echo reduced to a voice
  • Ovid’s version: Narcissus and his reflection
Pan
Goat-legged son of Hermes and Dryope, god of flocks and wild places, inventor of the panpipes; source of “panic.”
Echo
A nymph whose body faded until only her voice remained.
Silenus
An old, drunken satyr, tutor of Dionysus; in some accounts a son of Pan.
