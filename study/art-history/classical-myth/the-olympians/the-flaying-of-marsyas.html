Titian

The Flaying of Marsyas

ca. 1570–76

Oil on canvas · Archdiocesan Museum, Kroměříž

Made for: One of Titian’s last paintings, apparently kept in his studio and not made for a known patron.

The satyr Marsyas dared to challenge Apollo to a music contest, his pipes against the god’s lyre. He lost, and Apollo had him hung upside down and skinned alive. Titian’s loose, smeared brushwork makes the scene even more disturbing, while King Midas sits in thought nearby. The story warns against hubris, the human or near-human pride that challenges a god.

  • Music contest: pipes vs. lyre
