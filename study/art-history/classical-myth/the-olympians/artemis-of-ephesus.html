Artemis of Ephesus

Roman copy of a Hellenistic original of the 2nd century BCE

Marble and bronze · Capitoline Museums, Rome

Made for: A copy of the cult statue in Artemis’s great temple at Ephesus, one of the Seven Wonders of the ancient world.

At Ephesus, in Asia Minor, Artemis was worshipped as a mother goddess very unlike the slim huntress of mainland Greece. Her statue is covered with rows of rounded forms, long called breasts but possibly eggs or bulls’ parts, along with animals and bees, all symbols of fertility. The Ephesian Artemis shows how a Greek name could attach to an older local goddess.
