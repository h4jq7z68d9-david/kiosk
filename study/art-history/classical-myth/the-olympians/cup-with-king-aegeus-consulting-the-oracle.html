The Olympians · Zeus, Hera, Poseidon, and Apollo

Attributed to the Kodros Painter

Cup with King Aegeus consulting the oracle

Athenian, ca. 440–430 BCE

Red-figure kylix · Altes Museum, Berlin

Made for: A drinking cup; it is the only surviving image from Classical Athens of the Delphic oracle at work.

The Athenian king Aegeus, childless, asks the oracle what to do; the answer will lead to the birth of Theseus. The prophetess, labeled Themis here, sits on Apollo’s tall tripod holding a laurel sprig and a dish. At Delphi the priestess called the Pythia spoke Apollo’s answers, and priests turned them into verse.

  • Only Classical image of the Delphic oracle
  • Prophetess on Apollo’s tripod with laurel
  • Aegeus asks about an heir
Pythia
The priestess of Apollo at Delphi who spoke his prophecies, seated on a tripod.
Dactylic hexameter
The six-beat meter of epic poetry, in which Delphic answers were often delivered.
