The Olympians · Hermes, Hephaestus, Ares, and the Goddesses

Sandro Botticelli

Venus and Mars

ca. 1485

Tempera and oil on panel · National Gallery, London

Made for: A long, low panel probably set into the wall paneling or furniture of a Florentine bedroom, possibly for a wedding.

Aphrodite’s husband was Hephaestus, the lame smith whose forge was on the island of Lemnos, but she loved Ares. In the Odyssey, Hephaestus catches the lovers in an invisible net and calls the gods to laugh at them. Botticelli shows a calmer moral: Mars sleeps, exhausted, while Venus stays alert and little satyrs play with his armor. Love conquers war.

  • Aphrodite married to Hephaestus, loves Ares
  • Hephaestus’s net (Odyssey 8)
  • Love tames war
Lemnos
The island where Hephaestus landed when thrown from Olympus and where he kept a forge.
Children of Ares and Aphrodite
Phobos (panic), Deimos (fear), Harmonia (harmony), and in some accounts Eros (desire).

Themes

  • Desire as a dangerous power
