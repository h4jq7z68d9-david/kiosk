Gian Lorenzo Bernini

Apollo and Daphne

1622–25

Marble · Galleria Borghese, Rome · photographed in the 19th century

Made for: Carved for Cardinal Scipione Borghese’s villa, where it still stands; a Latin couplet on the base gives it a Christian moral about chasing fleeting pleasures.

Struck by one of Cupid’s arrows, Apollo chased the nymph Daphne, who prayed to escape and was turned into a laurel tree. Bernini catches the exact moment of change: bark climbs her legs, leaves burst from her fingers, and her toes root into the ground as Apollo reaches her. Apollo made the laurel his sacred plant, which is why winners and poets were crowned with laurel, and why we still say “laureate.”
