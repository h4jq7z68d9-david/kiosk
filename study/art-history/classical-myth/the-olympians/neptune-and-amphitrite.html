Jan Gossaert

Neptune and Amphitrite

1516

Oil on panel · Gemäldegalerie, Berlin

Made for: Painted for Philip of Burgundy, an admiral and later bishop, for his castle at Souburg; one of the first large mythological nudes in Northern art.

Poseidon, the Roman Neptune, married the sea nymph Amphitrite, and their son was the merman Triton. Gossaert, who had just seen ancient sculpture in Rome, poses the couple as massive classical nudes inside an ornate temple. Poseidon also fathered monsters and marvels: the Cyclops Polyphemus with the nymph Thoosa, and, with Medusa, the winged horse Pegasus and the warrior Chrysaor.

  • Poseidon marries Amphitrite; son Triton
  • Northern artist responds to ancient Rome
  • Father of Polyphemus and Pegasus
Amphitrite
Sea nymph, wife of Poseidon and mother of Triton.
Pegasus and Chrysaor
Offspring of Poseidon and Medusa, born from her neck when Perseus beheaded her; Bellerophon later tamed Pegasus.
Hades
Brother of Zeus who rules the dead with Persephone; the Cyclopes gave him a helmet of invisibility.
