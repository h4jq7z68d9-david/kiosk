Leda and the Swan

ca. 1505–07

Oil on panel · Uffizi Gallery, Florence · copy of Leonardo’s lost original

Made for: A copy by Leonardo’s pupil of a lost painting that was famous in its own time; copies spread the composition across Europe.

Zeus took the form of a swan to seduce Leda, queen of Sparta. From the eggs she laid came Helen, later of Troy, and in most versions her sister Clytemnestra and the twins Castor and Pollux. Leonardo’s original is lost, so copies like this one preserve his twisting standing figure and the children hatching at her feet.
