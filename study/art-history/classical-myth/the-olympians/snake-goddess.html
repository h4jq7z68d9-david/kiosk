“Snake Goddess”

Minoan, ca. 1600 BCE

Faience · Heraklion Archaeological Museum, Crete

Made for: Found in a storage pit at the palace of Knossos, probably a cult figure or offering.

This small figure holds a snake in each hand, with a cat perched on her head. Some scholars see Minoan goddesses like her as distant ancestors of Athena, who also has a sacred snake and a protective role over a city. Arthur Evans’s team restored it heavily, and parts, including the head and cat, may be modern.

  • Minoan figure holding snakes
  • Possible ancestor of Athena’s snake
  • Heavily restored by Evans
