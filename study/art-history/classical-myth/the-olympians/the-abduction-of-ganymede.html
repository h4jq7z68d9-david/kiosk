Correggio

The Abduction of Ganymede

ca. 1531–32

Oil on canvas · Kunsthistorisches Museum, Vienna

Made for: Part of a series on the Loves of Jupiter, probably made for Federico II Gonzaga, Duke of Mantua.

Zeus, in the form of an eagle, snatched up the beautiful Trojan prince Ganymede and carried him to Olympus to be the gods’ cupbearer. Correggio paints the boy rising into the sky while his dog barks below. The Romans called him Catamitus, the source of the English word “catamite,” because the story was read as Zeus’s love for a youth.
