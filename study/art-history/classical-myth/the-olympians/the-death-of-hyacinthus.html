The Olympians · Zeus, Hera, Poseidon, and Apollo

Giovanni Battista Tiepolo

The Death of Hyacinthus

1752–53

Oil on canvas · Thyssen-Bornemisza Museum, Madrid

Made for: Painted during Tiepolo’s stay in Würzburg; it belonged to Wilhelm Friedrich of Schaumburg-Lippe, who may have bought it directly from the artist.

Apollo loved the Spartan youth Hyacinthus and accidentally killed him with a thrown discus; from his blood grew the hyacinth flower. Tiepolo updates the story by replacing the discus with a tennis ball and racket at the youth’s feet. Apollo’s loves almost always end in loss: Cassandra rejects him, Coronis betrays him, Daphne flees.

  • Killed by Apollo’s discus
  • Blood becomes the hyacinth
  • Tiepolo swaps in a tennis racket
Cassandra
Trojan princess given prophecy by Apollo and then cursed never to be believed.
Cumaean Sibyl
Apollo’s prophetess at Cumae in Italy, who later guides Aeneas to the underworld.
Coronis and Asclepius
Coronis bore Apollo’s son Asclepius, god of medicine, who was raised by the centaur Chiron.
Hygieia
Daughter of Asclepius, goddess of health, source of the word “hygiene.”
