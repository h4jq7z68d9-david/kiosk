Sleeping Hermaphroditus

Roman copy, 2nd century CE, of a Hellenistic original

Marble; mattress added by Bernini in 1619 · Louvre, Paris

Made for: A Roman garden or villa sculpture designed to surprise: from the back it looks like a sleeping woman, from the front it reveals both sexes.

Hermaphroditus, the son of Hermes and Aphrodite, was so beautiful that the water nymph Salmacis begged the gods to join them forever, and their bodies fused into one with both male and female features. Another son of Aphrodite, Priapus (by Dionysus or Hermes), was a garden god of fertility. Bernini carved the startlingly soft mattress when the statue belonged to Cardinal Scipione Borghese.

  • Son of Hermes and Aphrodite
  • Fused with the nymph Salmacis
  • Bernini’s mattress, 1619
