The Olympians · Zeus, Hera, Poseidon, and Apollo

Titian

Danaë

ca. 1560–65

Oil on canvas · Museo del Prado, Madrid

Made for: A later version of the Danaë Titian sent to King Philip II of Spain in 1553 (now in the Wellington Collection, London); the first owner of this one is unknown.

King Acrisius of Argos locked his daughter Danaë in a bronze chamber because an oracle said her son would kill him. Zeus reached her as a shower of gold, and she gave birth to the hero Perseus. Titian contrasts the relaxed, glowing princess with her old nurse, who greedily catches the coins in her apron.
