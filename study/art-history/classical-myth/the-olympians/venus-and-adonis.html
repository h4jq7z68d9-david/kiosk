Titian

Venus and Adonis

1550s

Oil on canvas · Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Made for: One of several versions of a composition first painted for Philip II; Titian’s workshop repeated it for other buyers.

Adonis was born from a tree: his mother Myrrha (or Smyrna), after an incestuous passion for her father, King Cinyras of Cyprus, was turned into the myrrh tree. Aphrodite loved the beautiful young hunter and begged him not to hunt, but he was killed by a boar, and anemones sprang from his blood. Titian invents the moment of her clinging plea as he pulls away. Women mourned him each year in the festival of the Adonia.

  • Adonis born from the myrrh tree
  • Killed by a boar; anemones from his blood
  • Yearly mourning festival, the Adonia
Adonis
Beautiful youth loved by Aphrodite, killed by a boar; his cult mourned him as a dying god of vegetation.
Myrrha
Daughter of King Cinyras, turned into the myrrh tree; mother of Adonis.

Themes

  • Desire as a dangerous power
