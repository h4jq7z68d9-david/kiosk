The Olympians · Zeus, Hera, Poseidon, and Apollo

Diego Velázquez

Mercury and Argus

ca. 1659

Oil on canvas · Museo del Prado, Madrid

Made for: Painted for the Hall of Mirrors in the royal palace (Alcázar) of Madrid, a long, high room that explains the painting’s wide format.

Zeus sent Hermes to free Io. Hermes lulled the watchman Argus Panoptes (“all-seeing”) to sleep with music and stories, then killed him. Hera placed Argus’s hundred eyes on the tail of her peacock. Velázquez paints a quiet, shadowy moment as Hermes creeps toward the dozing guard, with the cow behind them.

  • Hermes lulls Argus to sleep
  • Argus’s eyes end up on the peacock
  • Late work by Velázquez
Argus Panoptes
Hundred-eyed giant Hera set to guard Io, killed by Hermes; his eyes decorate Hera’s peacock.
