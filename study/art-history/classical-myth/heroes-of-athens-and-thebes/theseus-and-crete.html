Heroes of Athens and Thebes

Theseus and Crete

Athens’ national hero, the Minotaur, and the Labyrinth

Athens claimed its people had sprung from its own soil, and its early kings were born from the earth. Its great hero was Theseus, who cleared the road to Athens of bandits, killed the Minotaur in Crete, and was remembered as the king who united Attica. His myths are tied to the island of Crete, where King Minos, the craftsman Daedalus, and the Labyrinth set the stage.

  • Athenians as “born from the earth”
  • Theseus: unifier and civic hero
  • Crete: Minos, Daedalus, the Minotaur
Autochthonous
“Sprung from the land itself,” the Athenian claim about their origins.
Cecrops
Legendary first king of Athens, half man and half snake, who judged the contest between Athena and Poseidon.
Erichthonius
Early Athenian king born from the earth after Hephaestus tried to assault Athena; raised secretly by Athena.

Theme

The hero of Athens

Athens built Theseus into its own Heracles.

As Athens grew powerful in the 500s and 400s BCE, it promoted Theseus as a civic hero who cleared the roads, freed the city from Cretan tribute, and united Attica under one government. His cycle of labors was arranged to match Heracles’, and his image spread on vases and temples. Myth gave the young democracy a founder it could be proud of.

  • Labors designed to rival Heracles’
  • Freed Athens from Cretan tribute
  • Founder figure for the democracy

Amazonomachy frieze from the Mausoleum at Halicarnassusca. 350 BCETheseus joined Heracles against the Amazons, warrior women from the edge of the world, and carried off their queen Antiope (sometimes called Hippolyta). The Amazons then invaded Athens and were defeated. Their son was Hippolytus. Greek artists loved the Amazonomachy as a battle of Greek men against a reversed world where women fight. Parthenon metope with a Lapith and a centaur447–438 BCEAt the wedding of Theseus’s friend Pirithous, king of the Lapiths, and Hippodamia, the drunken centaurs tried to carry off the bride and the women, and a great fight followed. Theseus and Pirithous later tried to kidnap brides of their own, Helen of Sparta and even Persephone; Helen’s brothers, Castor and Pollux, rescued her, and the two heroes were trapped in the underworld until Heracles freed Theseus. PhèdreAlexandre Cabanel · 1880Theseus later married Phaedra, Ariadne’s sister, who fell in love with her stepson Hippolytus. When he rejected her, she killed herself and left a letter accusing him. Theseus cursed his son, and Poseidon sent a bull from the sea that terrified Hippolytus’s horses and dragged him to his death. Cabanel shows Phaedra wasting away on her bed, consumed by forbidden love.
