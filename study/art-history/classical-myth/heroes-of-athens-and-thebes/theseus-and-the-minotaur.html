Heroes of Athens and Thebes · Theseus and Crete

Antonio Canova

Theseus and the Minotaur

1782

Marble · Victoria and Albert Museum, London

Made for: Canova’s first major work in Rome, carved from a block given by the Venetian ambassador Girolamo Zulian, who let him choose the subject; it became a manifesto of the new Neoclassical style.

Theseus volunteered to go to Crete with the tribute. Ariadne, Minos’s daughter, fell in love with him and gave him a ball of thread to find his way out of the Labyrinth. He killed the Minotaur and escaped with her. Canova shows the calm after the struggle: the hero sits quietly on the monster’s body, an image of restraint rather than violence.
