Jean-Auguste-Dominique Ingres

Oedipus and the Sphinx

1808 (enlarged 1827)

Oil on canvas · Louvre, Paris

Made for: A required study sent from Rome to the French Academy, later enlarged and sold; it made the nude hero a Neoclassical ideal.

Oedipus solved the riddle, the Sphinx killed herself, and grateful Thebes made him king and gave him the widowed queen Jocasta. They had four children: Antigone, Ismene, Polynices, and Eteocles. When plague struck, Oedipus vowed to find Laius’s killer and slowly discovered that it was himself, and that his wife was his mother. Jocasta hanged herself and Oedipus blinded himself. Ingres shows the calm, confident hero before he knows the truth.

  • Answers the riddle, becomes king
  • Discovers he killed his father, wed his mother
  • Blinds himself; Jocasta dies
