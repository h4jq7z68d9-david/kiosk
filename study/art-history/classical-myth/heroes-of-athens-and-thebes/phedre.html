Heroes of Athens and Thebes · Theseus and Crete

Alexandre Cabanel

Phèdre

1880

Oil on canvas · Musée Fabre, Montpellier

Made for: A Salon painting based on Racine’s tragedy Phèdre of 1677, itself drawn from Euripides.

Theseus later married Phaedra, Ariadne’s sister, who fell in love with her stepson Hippolytus. When he rejected her, she killed herself and left a letter accusing him. Theseus cursed his son, and Poseidon sent a bull from the sea that terrified Hippolytus’s horses and dragged him to his death. Cabanel shows Phaedra wasting away on her bed, consumed by forbidden love.

  • Phaedra loves her stepson Hippolytus
  • False accusation; Theseus’s curse
  • Euripides, then Racine
Phaedra
Daughter of Minos, wife of Theseus, who falsely accused her stepson.
Hippolytus
Son of Theseus and the Amazon queen, devoted to Artemis, killed by his father’s curse.
