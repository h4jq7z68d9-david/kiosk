Heroes of Athens and Thebes · Thebes, Oedipus, and Jason

Oedipus Painter

Cup with Oedipus and the Sphinx

Athenian, ca. 470 BCE

Red-figure kylix · Vatican Museums

Made for: A drinking cup for the symposium, with the picture inside the bowl.

An oracle warned King Laius of Thebes that his son would kill him, so he and Queen Jocasta exposed their baby with his ankles pinned, giving him the name Oedipus, “swollen foot.” A shepherd saved him, and he was raised by King Polybus and Queen Merope of Corinth. Fleeing a prophecy that he would kill his father, he killed a stranger at a crossroads: Laius. At Thebes he answered the Sphinx’s riddle, which this cup shows him pondering.

  • Exposed as a baby, raised in Corinth
  • Kills Laius at a crossroads
  • Ponders the Sphinx’s riddle
Laius and Jocasta
King and queen of Thebes, Oedipus’s parents.
Polybus and Merope
King and queen of Corinth who raised Oedipus as their son.
Sphinx
A winged lion with a woman’s head who killed all who failed her riddle.
Riddle of the Sphinx
What walks on four legs in the morning, two at noon, and three in the evening? A human: crawling, walking, then with a cane.

Themes

  • Tragedy: knowledge that destroys
