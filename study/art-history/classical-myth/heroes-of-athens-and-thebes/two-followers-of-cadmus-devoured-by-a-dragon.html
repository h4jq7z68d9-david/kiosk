Heroes of Athens and Thebes · Thebes, Oedipus, and Jason

Cornelis van Haarlem

Two Followers of Cadmus Devoured by a Dragon

1588

Oil on canvas · National Gallery, London

Made for: A dramatic showpiece of Dutch Mannerism; Hendrick Goltzius made a famous engraving after it the same year, which spread the design.

Cadmus, searching for his sister Europa, was told by Delphi to found a city where a cow lay down. When his men went for water, a dragon sacred to Ares killed them; Cadmus slew it and, on Athena’s advice, sowed its teeth, from which armed men sprang up and fought until five remained, the Spartoi, ancestors of Thebes’ nobles. Van Haarlem shows the dragon tearing into the companions in the foreground while Cadmus fights in the distance.

  • Cadmus follows a cow to found Thebes
  • Dragon of Ares kills his men
  • Sown teeth become the Spartoi
Cadmus
Phoenician prince who founded Thebes; he married Harmonia, daughter of Ares and Aphrodite.
Spartoi
“Sown men,” warriors grown from the dragon’s teeth.
Harmonia
Daughter of Ares and Aphrodite, wife of Cadmus; her cursed necklace brought ruin to their descendants.
Amphion and Zethus
Twin sons of Zeus and Antiope who built Thebes’ walls; Amphion married Niobe.
Dirce
Queen who mistreated Antiope and was tied to a bull by the twins.
