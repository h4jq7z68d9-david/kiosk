Bertel Thorvaldsen

Jason with the Golden Fleece

1802–03 (marble 1828)

Marble · Thorvaldsens Museum, Copenhagen

Made for: The model that made the Danish sculptor famous in Rome; the marble was commissioned by the English banker Thomas Hope.

The Golden Fleece came from a flying ram sent to rescue Phrixus and Helle, children of King Athamas, from their stepmother Ino; Helle fell into the sea (the Hellespont), and Phrixus reached Colchis, where King Aeetes hung the fleece in a grove guarded by a dragon. Jason, whose uncle Pelias had taken the throne of Iolcus, was sent to fetch it. Thorvaldsen shows him striding home with the fleece over his arm, modeled on ancient statues.

  • Ram rescues Phrixus and Helle
  • Pelias sends Jason for the fleece
  • Neoclassical hero modeled on ancient statues
Phrixus and Helle
Children of Athamas and Nephele saved by the golden ram; Helle fell into the Hellespont.
Aeetes
King of Colchis, son of Helios, keeper of the fleece and father of Medea.
Pelias
Jason’s uncle, who seized the throne of Iolcus.
Argo and Argonauts
The ship built by Argus, and the heroes who sailed in her, including Heracles, Orpheus, Castor, and Pollux.
