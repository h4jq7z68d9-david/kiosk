Heroes of Athens and Thebes · Thebes, Oedipus, and Jason

Gustave Moreau

Jason

1865

Oil on canvas · Musée d’Orsay, Paris

Made for: Shown at the Paris Salon of 1865, where Moreau’s jewel-like, dreamlike myths set him apart from the realists of his day.

The Golden Fleece came from a flying ram sent to rescue Phrixus and Helle, children of King Athamas, from their stepmother Ino; Phrixus reached Colchis, where King Aeetes hung the fleece in a grove guarded by a dragon. Jason, whose uncle Pelias had taken the throne of Iolcus, was sent to fetch it. Moreau shows him standing over the dead dragon, fleece in hand, while Medea, whose magic made it possible, stands close behind.

  • Ram rescues Phrixus and Helle
  • Pelias sends Jason for the fleece
  • Medea’s magic wins the fleece
Phrixus and Helle
Children of Athamas and Nephele saved by the golden ram; Helle fell into the Hellespont.
Aeetes
King of Colchis, son of Helios, keeper of the fleece and father of Medea.
Pelias
Jason’s uncle, who seized the throne of Iolcus.
Argo and Argonauts
The ship built by Argus, and the heroes who sailed in her, including Heracles, Orpheus, Castor, and Pollux.
