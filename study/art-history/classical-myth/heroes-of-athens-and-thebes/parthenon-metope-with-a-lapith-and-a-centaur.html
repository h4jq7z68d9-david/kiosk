Heroes of Athens and Thebes · Theseus and Crete

Parthenon metope with a Lapith and a centaur

447–438 BCE

Marble, South Metope 31 · British Museum, London

Made for: One of the carved panels on the south side of the Parthenon, the temple of Athena on the Acropolis.

At the wedding of Theseus’s friend Pirithous, king of the Lapiths, and Hippodamia, the drunken centaurs tried to carry off the bride and the women, and a great fight followed. Theseus and Pirithous later tried to kidnap brides of their own, Helen of Sparta and even Persephone; Helen’s brothers, Castor and Pollux, rescued her, and the two heroes were trapped in the underworld until Heracles freed Theseus.

  • Centaurs attack the Lapith wedding
  • Theseus and Pirithous seek divine brides
  • Trapped in Hades; Heracles rescues Theseus
Centauromachy
The battle of the Lapiths and centaurs, an image of reason against animal passion.
Pirithous and Hippodamia
King of the Lapiths and his bride, whose wedding the centaurs disrupted.
Castor and Pollux
Twin brothers of Helen (the Dioscuri) who rescued her from Theseus.
