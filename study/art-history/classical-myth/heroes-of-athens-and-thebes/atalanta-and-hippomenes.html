Guido Reni

Atalanta and Hippomenes

ca. 1620–25

Oil on canvas · Museo di Capodimonte, Naples

Made for: A large mythological painting; Reni painted two versions, the other now in the Prado, Madrid.

Atalanta, a swift huntress who wished to stay unmarried, raced every suitor and killed those she beat. Hippomenes (or Melanion) prayed to Aphrodite, who gave him three golden apples, from the Garden of the Hesperides or, in Ovid, from her own sacred grove on Cyprus. During the race he dropped them one by one, and Atalanta stopped to pick them up and lost. Reni turns the race into a slow, elegant dance of two pale bodies.
