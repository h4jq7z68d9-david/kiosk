Antigone Gives Token Burial to the Body of Her Brother Polynices

1835–98

Watercolor, pen and ink over black chalk · Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Made for: A drawing by a French academic history painter; the museum does not record what it was made for.

After Oedipus left Thebes, his sons agreed to share the throne, but Eteocles refused to give it up. Polynices raised an army, the Seven against Thebes, and the brothers killed each other. Their uncle Creon, now king, forbade burial of the traitor Polynices. Their sister Antigone buried him anyway, choosing the laws of the gods over the king’s command, and was sentenced to be walled up alive.

  • Brothers kill each other: the Seven against Thebes
  • Creon forbids burial
  • Antigone obeys the gods, not the king
Seven against Thebes
The seven champions who attacked Thebes on behalf of Polynices.
Creon
Jocasta’s brother, king of Thebes after the war, who condemned Antigone.
