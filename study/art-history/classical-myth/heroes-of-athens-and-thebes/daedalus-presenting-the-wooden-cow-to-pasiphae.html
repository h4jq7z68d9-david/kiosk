Heroes of Athens and Thebes · Theseus and Crete

Daedalus presenting the wooden cow to Pasiphaë

Roman, 62–79 CE

Wall painting · House of the Vettii, Pompeii

Made for: Part of the painted decoration of a reception room in a wealthy Pompeian house, paired with other myths of tragic love.

Minos, son of Zeus and Europa, became king of Crete. When he refused to sacrifice a magnificent bull to Poseidon, the god made his wife Pasiphaë desire it. The craftsman Daedalus built her a hollow wooden cow, and she bore the Minotaur, half man and half bull, which Minos shut inside the Labyrinth that Daedalus designed. Athens had to send seven youths and seven maidens to feed it.

  • Pasiphaë cursed with desire for a bull
  • Daedalus builds the wooden cow
  • Minotaur kept in the Labyrinth
Minos and Pasiphaë
King and queen of Crete; their children include Ariadne, Phaedra, and Androgeus.
Daedalus
Athenian master craftsman who built the cow, the Labyrinth, and wings.
Talos
A bronze giant who guarded Crete by circling it three times a day.
Androgeus
Son of Minos killed in Attica; his death led Minos to demand the human tribute from Athens.
