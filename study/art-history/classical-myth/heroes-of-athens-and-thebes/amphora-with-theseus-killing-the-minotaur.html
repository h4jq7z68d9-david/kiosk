Heroes of Athens and Thebes · Theseus and Crete

Amphora with Theseus killing the Minotaur

Athenian, mid-6th century BCE

Black-figure terracotta · Louvre, Paris

Made for: A storage jar painted with Athens’ favorite hero, popular as the city promoted Theseus in the 500s BCE.

Theseus was the son of King Aegeus of Athens (or of Poseidon) and Aethra of Troezen. Growing up, he lifted a boulder to find his father’s sword and sandals and walked to Athens, killing bandits on the way: the clubber Periphetes, Sinis the pine-bender, the sow of Crommyon, Sciron, the wrestler Cercyon, and Procrustes, who stretched or cut guests to fit his bed. At Athens Medea tried to poison him before Aegeus recognized the sword.

  • Son of Aegeus (or Poseidon) and Aethra
  • Six bandits on the road, then the Minotaur
  • Recognized by his father’s sword
Seven labors of Theseus
Periphetes, Sinis, the Crommyonian Sow, Sciron, Cercyon, Procrustes, and the Minotaur.
Medea
Sorceress from Colchis, then wife of Aegeus, who tried to poison Theseus.
Procrustes
Bandit who forced travelers to fit his bed by stretching or cutting them.
