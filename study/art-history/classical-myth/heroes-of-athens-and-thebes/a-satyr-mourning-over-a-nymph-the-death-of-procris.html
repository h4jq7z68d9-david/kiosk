Piero di Cosimo

A Satyr Mourning over a Nymph (The Death of Procris)

ca. 1495

Oil on panel · National Gallery, London

Made for: A long panel probably set into the wall paneling or furniture of a Florentine house.

The Athenian princess Procris married the hunter Cephalus, who was carried off for a time by Eos, the dawn goddess. Their marriage was poisoned by mutual testing and jealousy. Procris gave him a hound, Laelaps, that always caught its prey, and a spear that never missed; spying on him one day, she was killed by the spear when he mistook her for an animal. Piero shows a gentle satyr and the dog grieving over her body.

  • Cephalus, Procris, and Eos
  • Jealousy and a spear that never misses
  • Satyr and hound mourn her
Procris and Cephalus
Athenian couple whose jealousy ended when Cephalus killed Procris by accident.
Laelaps
A hound that always caught its prey; in some versions Minos gave it to Procris.
Eos (Aurora)
Goddess of the dawn, who carried off Cephalus.
