Heroes of Athens and Thebes · Thebes, Oedipus, and Jason

Eugène Delacroix

Medea about to Kill Her Children

1838

Oil on canvas · Palais des Beaux-Arts, Lille

Made for: A Salon painting bought by the French state, a showpiece of Romantic drama.

On the voyage the Argonauts met the women of Lemnos, King Cyzicus, the boxer Amycus, and the blind prophet Phineus, whom the winged brothers Zetes and Calais freed from the Harpies; Heracles was left behind searching for his beloved Hylas. In Colchis, Aeetes’ daughter Medea used her magic to help Jason yoke fire-breathing bulls and take the fleece, and on the way home she brought down the bronze giant Talos. Years later Jason abandoned her for a Corinthian princess, Glauce, and Medea killed the bride and her own children. Delacroix shows her hiding in a cave, dagger in hand, as her pursuers approach.

Medea
Colchian princess and sorceress who helped Jason and took revenge when he left her.
Phineus and the Harpies
Blind prophet tormented by Harpies who snatched his food until the Argonauts drove them off.
Hylas
Heracles’ young companion, pulled into a spring by nymphs.
Talos and ichor
Bronze giant of Crete whose single vein of ichor, the gods’ blood, Medea drained.
Glauce
Corinthian princess Jason planned to marry, killed by Medea’s poisoned robe.
