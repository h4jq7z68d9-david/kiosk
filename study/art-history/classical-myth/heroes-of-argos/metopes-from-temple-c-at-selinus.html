Heroes of Argos · Perseus and the Heroes of Argos

Metopes from Temple C at Selinus

Greek (Sicily), ca. 540–530 BCE

Limestone · Regional Archaeological Museum, Palermo

Made for: Carved panels from the frieze of a temple, probably to Apollo, in the Greek colony of Selinus in Sicily.

The center metope shows Perseus cutting off Medusa’s head while Athena stands by and Medusa clutches the winged horse Pegasus. The Gorgons were three sisters: Stheno (“strength”), Euryale (“far-leaper”), and the mortal Medusa (“ruler”). Perseus used the gifts of the gods, winged sandals, a cap of invisibility, a curved sword, and a bag, and looked only at her reflection to avoid being turned to stone. From her severed neck sprang Pegasus and Chrysaor, her sons by Poseidon.

  • Perseus beheads Medusa with Athena’s help
  • Gifts: sandals, cap, sword, bag
  • Pegasus and Chrysaor spring from her neck
Gorgons
Stheno, Euryale, and Medusa, snake-haired sisters whose gaze turns people to stone; only Medusa was mortal.
Metope
A square panel, often carved, set between the grooved blocks of a Doric temple frieze.
