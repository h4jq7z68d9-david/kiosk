Heroes of Argos · Heracles

François Lemoyne

The Apotheosis of Hercules

1733–36

Oil on canvas mounted on the ceiling · Salon d’Hercule, Palace of Versailles

Made for: A huge ceiling painting for a reception room at Versailles, flattering King Louis XV as a heroic, god-like ruler.

As Heracles’ body burned on the pyre, his mortal part was consumed and his divine part rose to Olympus. There he was reconciled with Hera and married her daughter Hebe, goddess of youth. Lemoyne fills the ceiling with more than a hundred figures welcoming the hero into heaven. Rulers loved the theme because it promised that great labors earn immortality.

  • Pyre on Mount Oeta
  • Rises to Olympus; marries Hebe
  • Royal symbol of earned glory
Apotheosis
The raising of a mortal to the status of a god.
Hebe
Goddess of youth, daughter of Zeus and Hera, Heracles’ wife on Olympus.

Themes

  • The flawed hero
