Heroes of Argos · Perseus and the Heroes of Argos

Edward Burne-Jones

Perseus and the Graiae

ca. 1875–78

Gesso relief with gold and silver leaf on oak panel · National Museum Cardiff

Made for: One panel of a Perseus cycle commissioned by the politician Arthur Balfour for the music room of his London house.

Danaë and the infant Perseus were set adrift in a chest and washed ashore on the island of Seriphos, where the fisherman Dictys took them in. King Polydectes, wanting Danaë, sent Perseus to fetch the head of the Gorgon Medusa. To find her, Perseus seized the single eye and tooth shared by the Graiae, the Gorgons’ gray-haired sisters, until they gave him directions. Burne-Jones shows him snatching the eye as the three blind sisters grope for it.

Dictys and Polydectes
Brothers on Seriphos: the kind fisherman who rescued Danaë, and the king who sent Perseus away so he could force her into marriage.
Graiae
“The gray ones,” three old sisters of the Gorgons who share one eye and one tooth.
