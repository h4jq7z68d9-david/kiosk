Titian

Perseus and Andromeda

1554–56

Oil on canvas · Wallace Collection, London

Made for: One of the poesie, mythological paintings Titian made for King Philip II of Spain.

On his way home, Perseus found the Ethiopian princess Andromeda chained to a rock by the sea. Her mother, Queen Cassiopeia, had boasted that she was more beautiful than the Nereids, the sea nymphs, and Poseidon sent a sea monster to punish the kingdom; an oracle said only Andromeda’s sacrifice would end it. Titian shows Perseus diving down from the sky while the pale, chained princess twists away from the monster below.

  • Cassiopeia’s boast offends the Nereids
  • Andromeda chained as a sacrifice
  • Perseus swoops down to save her
Cepheus and Cassiopeia
King and queen of Ethiopia, parents of Andromeda; all three became constellations.
Nereids
Sea nymphs, the fifty daughters of Nereus, including Amphitrite and Thetis.
Ceto
A sea monster (or the sea goddess who mothered monsters) sent to attack Ethiopia.
