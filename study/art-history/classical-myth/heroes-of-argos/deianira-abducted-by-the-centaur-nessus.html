Guido Reni

Deianira Abducted by the Centaur Nessus

1620–21

Oil on canvas · Louvre, Paris

Made for: One of four scenes from Heracles’ life painted for Ferdinando Gonzaga, Duke of Mantua, for his villa.

Heracles’ second wife, Deianira, was carried off by the centaur Nessus, who offered to ferry her across a river; Heracles shot him with an arrow dipped in the Hydra’s poison. The dying centaur told Deianira to keep his blood as a love charm. Years later, jealous of a captive princess, she sent Heracles a robe soaked in it, and the poison burned his flesh. In agony he built his own funeral pyre on Mount Oeta.

  • Nessus seizes Deianira
  • Poisoned blood as a false love charm
  • The burning robe kills Heracles
