Heroes of Argos · Heracles

Metope with Heracles, Atlas, and Athena

ca. 470–456 BCE

Marble · Archaeological Museum of Olympia

Made for: One of twelve metopes of the labors on the Temple of Zeus at Olympia, the sanctuary Heracles was said to have founded.

The later labors took Heracles to the edges of the world: the Cretan Bull, the man-eating Mares of Diomedes, the belt of the Amazon queen Hippolyta, the cattle of the monster Geryon, and the golden apples of the Hesperides. For the apples, Heracles held up the sky while Atlas fetched them. Here Atlas returns with the apples while Athena quietly helps Heracles bear the weight.

  • Labors 7–11 at the ends of the earth
  • Heracles holds the sky for Atlas
  • Athena helps him from behind
Labors 7–11
The Cretan Bull, the Mares of Diomedes, the Belt of the Amazon Hippolyta, the Cattle of Geryon, and the Apples of the Hesperides.
Pillars of Heracles
The rocks at the Strait of Gibraltar, said to have been set up by Heracles on his way to Geryon.
Alcestis
Queen whom Heracles rescued by wrestling Death, a side-deed on his way to the Mares of Diomedes.
