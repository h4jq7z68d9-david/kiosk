Heroes of Argos · Heracles

Euphronios

Krater with Heracles wrestling Antaeus

Athenian, ca. 515–510 BCE

Red-figure calyx krater · Louvre, Paris

Made for: A large bowl for mixing wine and water at a symposium, by one of the first great red-figure painters.

On his way to the apples of the Hesperides, Heracles fought Antaeus, a giant son of Earth who grew stronger every time he touched the ground. Heracles lifted him into the air and crushed him. Euphronios contrasts the neat, controlled hero with the wild-haired giant whose eyes roll in pain, a picture of civilization defeating savagery.

  • Antaeus draws strength from Earth
  • Heracles lifts and crushes him
  • Early red-figure anatomy
Antaeus
Libyan giant, son of Gaea, invincible while touching the earth.
