Farnese Hercules

Roman copy, early 3rd century CE, of a Greek bronze of the 4th century BCE

Marble, signed by Glykon of Athens · National Archaeological Museum, Naples · photograph by Giorgio Sommer, 1860s

Made for: A colossal statue made for the Baths of Caracalla in Rome, where bathers would see the hero resting after his labors.

The hero leans exhausted on his club, draped with the skin of the Nemean lion. Behind his back he holds the golden apples of the Hesperides, so this is the end of his labors, not the beginning. Hera sent snakes to kill him in his cradle, but the infant strangled them; later she drove him mad, and he killed his first wife, Megara, and their children. To be cleansed of this blood guilt he served Eurystheus.

  • Resting hero with club and lion skin
  • Golden apples hidden behind his back
  • Labors as penance for killing his family
Megara
Heracles’ first wife, killed with their children when Hera drove him mad.
Miasma
The blood pollution Heracles had to wash away through his labors.
