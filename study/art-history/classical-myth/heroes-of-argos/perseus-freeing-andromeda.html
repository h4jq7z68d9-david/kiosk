Heroes of Argos · Perseus and the Heroes of Argos

Peter Paul Rubens

Perseus Freeing Andromeda

ca. 1620–22

Oil on canvas · Hermitage Museum, St. Petersburg

Made for: Rubens probably kept it in his own house in Antwerp until his death; it reached the Hermitage in 1769.

Rubens skips the fight and shows the aftermath: Perseus, crowned by Victory, gently frees Andromeda while cupids lead Pegasus and the dead monster lies below. Perseus later used Medusa’s head to turn Polydectes and his court to stone, returned the gifts of the gods, and, as foretold, accidentally killed his grandfather Acrisius with a discus. He then ruled Tiryns, and his granddaughter Alcmena’s son was Heracles.

  • Perseus frees Andromeda, crowned by Victory
  • Petrifies Polydectes; kills Acrisius by accident
  • Founds the royal line of Heracles
Andromeda
Ethiopian princess rescued by Perseus and later his wife; a constellation.

Themes
