Caravaggio

Medusa

ca. 1597–98

Oil on canvas mounted on a wooden shield · Uffizi Gallery, Florence

Made for: A ceremonial parade shield, given by Cardinal Francesco del Monte to Ferdinando I de’ Medici, Grand Duke of Tuscany.

Even after death, Medusa’s head kept its power, and Perseus gave it to Athena, who set it on her aegis. Images of the Gorgon’s face were placed on shields, armor, and temples to frighten away enemies and evil. Caravaggio paints the head at the instant of beheading, still screaming, on a real convex shield, so the viewer, like Perseus, sees her only as a reflection.
