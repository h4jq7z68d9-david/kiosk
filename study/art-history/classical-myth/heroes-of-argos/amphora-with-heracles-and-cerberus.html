Heroes of Argos · Heracles

Andokides Painter

Amphora with Heracles and Cerberus

Athenian, ca. 520–510 BCE

Red-figure terracotta · Louvre, Paris

Made for: A storage jar decorated by one of the painters credited with inventing the red-figure technique.

The last and hardest labor was to bring Cerberus, the three-headed guard dog of the underworld, up to the living. Heracles crouches and reaches out to the dog almost gently, holding a chain, while Athena stands behind him. When Heracles brought Cerberus to Eurystheus, the frightened king hid in a storage jar. Going down to Hades and returning was the hero’s ultimate victory over death.

  • Labor 12: capture Cerberus
  • Heracles approaches the dog with a chain
  • A hero who returns from death
Red-figure
Greek vase technique (from ca. 530 BCE) leaving figures in the red clay against a black background, so details could be painted with a brush.

Themes

  • Labors that make the world safe
