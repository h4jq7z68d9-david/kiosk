Heroes of Argos · Heracles

Annibale Carracci

The Choice of Hercules

1596

Oil on canvas · Museo di Capodimonte, Naples

Made for: The central ceiling painting of the private study (Camerino) of Cardinal Odoardo Farnese in his Roman palace.

In a story told by the Greek writer Prodicus, the young Heracles meets two women at a crossroads: Pleasure offers an easy life, while Virtue points to a steep, rocky path that leads to glory. Heracles chooses Virtue. Carracci set the scene above the desk of a young cardinal, as a reminder to choose duty over pleasure.

  • Pleasure vs. Virtue at a crossroads
  • Heracles chooses the hard road
  • Moral lesson for a young cardinal
Psychomachia
A “battle for the soul” between virtues and vices; Heracles at the crossroads became a favorite image of it.

Themes

  • The flawed hero
