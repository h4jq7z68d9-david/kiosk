Theater of Epidaurus

ca. 340–300 BCE

Limestone · Epidaurus, Greece

Made for: A theater in the sanctuary of the healing god Asclepius, famous for its acoustics and still used for performances.

Greek drama grew out of festivals for Dionysus, and in Athens tragedies and comedies were performed at his festival, the City Dionysia. A theater had a curved seating area (theatron), a round dancing floor for the chorus (orchestra), and a stage building behind (skene). Epidaurus, the best preserved, holds about 13,000 people.

  • Drama born in Dionysus’s festivals
  • Theatron, orchestra, skene
  • Best-preserved Greek theater
