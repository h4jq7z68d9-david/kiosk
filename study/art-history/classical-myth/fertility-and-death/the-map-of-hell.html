Fertility and Death · Myths of Death

Sandro Botticelli

The Map of Hell

ca. 1480–95

Ink and color on parchment · Vatican Library

Made for: Part of a lavish illustrated manuscript of Dante’s Divine Comedy, probably for Lorenzo di Pierfrancesco de’ Medici.

Plato’s Republic ends with the Myth of Er, a soldier who returns from death to describe souls being judged, rewarded, punished, and choosing new lives. Ideas like these flowed into Christian visions of the afterlife. Dante’s Inferno arranges hell as a funnel of nine circles, staffed by classical figures such as Charon, Minos, and Cerberus, which Botticelli maps from top to bottom.

  • Plato’s Myth of Er: judgment and rebirth
  • Dante: hell as nine circles
  • Classical guardians in a Christian hell
