The Bacchanal of the Andrians

1523–26

Oil on canvas · Museo del Prado, Madrid

Made for: Painted for Alfonso d’Este, Duke of Ferrara, as a companion to Bacchus and Ariadne in his private study.

Based on an ancient description of a painting by Philostratus, the scene shows the people of the island of Andros, where a river ran with wine thanks to Dionysus. Dancers, drinkers, and a sleeping nude enjoy a festival of the god. A sheet of music in the foreground reads, in French, that whoever drinks and does not drink again does not know what drinking is.

  • Island of Andros with a river of wine
  • Based on an ancient text by Philostratus
  • Painted for the Duke of Ferrara
Bacchanal
A festival or revel in honor of Bacchus; Rome’s Bacchanalia were banned in 186 BCE.
