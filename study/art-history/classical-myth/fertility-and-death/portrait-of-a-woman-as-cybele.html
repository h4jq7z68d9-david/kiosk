Portrait of a woman as Cybele

Roman, ca. 50 CE

Marble · J. Paul Getty Museum, Los Angeles

Made for: A portrait of a Roman woman, possibly a priestess or a member of the imperial family, shown in the guise of the goddess.

Cybele, the Great Mother of Phrygia in Asia Minor, sits enthroned with a lion and a tambourine. Her young consort Attis, born when the nymph Nana conceived from an almond (a fruit of the monster Agdestis), castrated himself in a frenzy and died, and her priests imitated him. Rome officially adopted Cybele in 204 BCE. Her cult, like that of Adonis, centered on a dying young god mourned by a goddess.

  • Phrygian Great Mother, with lions
  • Consort Attis dies and is mourned
  • Adopted by Rome in 204 BCE
Cybele
Great Mother goddess of Phrygia, shown with lions and a turreted crown.
Attis
Cybele’s young consort who castrated himself and died; mourned in her rites.
Agdestis
A double-sexed monster in Attis’s myth whose castration produced the almond tree.
Nana
Daughter of a river god who conceived Attis from an almond.
Cult of Adonis
Women’s yearly mourning for Aphrodite’s dead lover, a vegetation god who dies young.
