Joachim Patinir

Charon Crossing the Styx

ca. 1520–24

Oil on panel · Museo del Prado, Madrid

Made for: A landscape painting whose first owner is not recorded; it later belonged to Philip II of Spain.

Patinir places Charon’s boat in the middle of a vast river between paradise on the left, with angels, and hell on the right, guarded by the three-headed dog Cerberus. The small soul in the boat looks toward hell. The painting mixes the classical ferryman with the Christian choice between salvation and damnation.

  • Charon’s boat between two shores
  • Paradise vs. hell, guarded by Cerberus
  • Classical myth meets Christian judgment
