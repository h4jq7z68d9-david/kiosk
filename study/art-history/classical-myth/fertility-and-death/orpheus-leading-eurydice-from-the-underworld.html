Fertility and Death · Myths of Death

Jean-Baptiste-Camille Corot

Orpheus Leading Eurydice from the Underworld

1861

Oil on canvas · Museum of Fine Arts, Houston

Made for: A large Salon landscape with a mythological subject, made for public exhibition.

Corot imagines the underworld as a misty, silvery grove, closer to the Elysian Fields than to a place of torment. Orpheus raises his lyre and leads Eurydice by the hand, not yet looking back. In the Greek imagination most souls drifted through dim Asphodel Meadows, while a lucky few heroes went to Elysium, a paradise of endless calm.

  • Underworld as a misty grove
  • Orpheus not yet looking back
  • Asphodel Meadows vs. Elysium
Asphodel Meadows
The gray plain where ordinary souls wander.
Elysium (Elysian Fields)
The paradise reserved for heroes and the favored of the gods.
