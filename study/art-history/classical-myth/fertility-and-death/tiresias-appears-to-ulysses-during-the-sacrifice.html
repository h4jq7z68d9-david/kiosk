Henry Fuseli

Tiresias Appears to Ulysses during the Sacrifice

1780–85

Watercolor · Albertina, Vienna

Made for: A large watercolor by the Swiss-born painter, who settled in London and specialized in dramatic scenes from Homer, Shakespeare, and Milton.

In Odyssey 11, the witch Circe sends Odysseus to the edge of the world, the land of the Cimmerians, to consult the dead seer Tiresias. He digs a pit and pours in sacrificial blood, and the shades flock to drink it. Fuseli shows the blind prophet rising in a cloud of shades while Odysseus crouches by the pit, sword drawn. Odysseus’s visit is an act of necromancy, calling up the dead to learn the future.

  • Circe sends Odysseus to the dead
  • Blood offering draws the shades
  • Tiresias foretells his journey home
Tiresias
The blind Theban prophet who keeps his wisdom even in death.
Circe
Witch-goddess who sends Odysseus to consult the dead.
Necromancer
One who calls up the dead to ask about the future.
Cimmerians
A people living in perpetual mist at the edge of the world, near the entrance to Hades.

Themes

  • A gloomy afterlife for almost everyone
