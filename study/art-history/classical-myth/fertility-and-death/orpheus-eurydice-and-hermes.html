Orpheus, Eurydice, and Hermes

Roman copy, 1st century BCE, of an Athenian relief of ca. 410 BCE

Marble · Louvre, Paris

Made for: A Roman copy of a famous Athenian relief, made for a collector who wanted a celebrated Greek work.

The poet Orpheus, whose music could move stones, went down to Hades to bring back his wife Eurydice, who had died from a snakebite. Charmed by his song, the gods let her go on one condition: he must not look back. He turned at the last moment, and she was lost. The relief shows the farewell, as Hermes gently takes her wrist to lead her back.

  • Orpheus charms the underworld
  • He looks back and loses Eurydice
  • Hermes leads her away
Orpheus and Eurydice
The singer who almost rescued his wife from death but looked back too soon.
Orphism
A religious movement claiming Orpheus as founder, teaching purity and the soul’s rebirth.
Metempsychosis
The transmigration of the soul into a new body after death, taught by Orphics and Pythagoreans.
