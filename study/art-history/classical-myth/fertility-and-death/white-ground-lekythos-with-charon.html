Fertility and Death · Myths of Death

White-ground lekythos with Charon

Athenian, later 5th century BCE

Terracotta · Museum für Kunst und Gewerbe, Hamburg

Made for: An oil flask painted for the dead: lekythoi with white grounds were left at graves and buried with the deceased.

Hermes, as guide of souls, led the dead to the river, where the ferryman Charon rowed them across for the price of a coin. Families placed an obol in the mouth of the dead for the fare. White-ground lekythoi, painted in delicate colors on a chalky white surface, show these last journeys and quiet farewells at the tomb.

  • Hermes leads, Charon ferries
  • Coin for the fare
  • Grave offerings for the dead
Psychopompos
“Guide of souls,” Hermes’s role in leading the dead.
Charon
The ferryman who carries the dead across the river of the underworld.
Lekythos
A narrow oil flask; white-ground lekythoi were funeral offerings.
