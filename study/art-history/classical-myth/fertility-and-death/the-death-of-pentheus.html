The Death of Pentheus

Roman, 62–79 CE

Wall painting · House of the Vettii, Pompeii

Made for: Part of the painted decoration of a dining room in a wealthy freedmen’s house, where mythological scenes impressed guests.

King Pentheus of Thebes, Dionysus’s cousin, refused to accept the new god and tried to suppress his rites. In Euripides’ tragedy the Bacchae, Dionysus lures him to spy on the maenads, who in their frenzy mistake him for a beast and tear him apart, led by his own mother, Agave. The painting shows the women closing in as Pentheus falls.
