Titian

Sisyphus

1548–49

Oil on canvas · Museo del Prado, Madrid

Made for: One of four paintings of the great sinners (the “Furies”) made for Mary of Hungary’s palace at Binche, as a warning to rebels.

A few sinners earned eternal punishment in Tartarus. Sisyphus, who cheated death, pushes a boulder uphill forever; Tantalus stands in water that recedes when he bends to drink, beneath fruit he cannot reach; Ixion, who tried to seduce Hera, spins on a fiery wheel; and Tityus has his liver eaten by vultures. Titian’s Sisyphus bends under a rock that seems to crush him.

  • Sisyphus: endless boulder
  • Tantalus, Ixion, Tityus
  • Painted as warnings to rebels
Tartarus
The deepest part of the underworld, where the worst sinners are punished.
Tantalus
Tormented with hunger and thirst, food and water always out of reach; source of “tantalize.”
Sisyphus
King who cheated death and must push a boulder uphill forever.
Ixion
Bound to a spinning wheel for trying to seduce Hera; from his union with a cloud came the Centaurs.
Centaurs
Half-human, half-horse creatures, descendants of Ixion.

Themes

  • Punishments that fit the crime
