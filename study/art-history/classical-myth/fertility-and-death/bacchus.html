Fertility and Death · Demeter and Dionysus

Caravaggio

Bacchus

ca. 1596–98

Oil on canvas · Uffizi Gallery, Florence

Made for: Probably painted for Cardinal Francesco del Monte, Caravaggio’s early patron, and given to the Medici.

Dionysus was the son of Zeus and the Theban princess Semele, who was burned up when Zeus showed himself in full glory; Zeus sewed the unborn child into his thigh and gave birth to him there. Semele, brought up from Hades and made immortal, was renamed Thyone. Caravaggio paints a flushed young model in a toga, offering a glass of wine beside a bowl of overripe fruit.

  • Twice born: from Semele and from Zeus’s thigh
  • Semele becomes the goddess Thyone
  • Caravaggio’s down-to-earth god
Semele (Thyone)
Theban princess, mother of Dionysus, destroyed by Zeus’s lightning and later deified as Thyone.
Ino (Leucothea) and Athamas
Semele’s sister and her husband, who nursed the infant Dionysus; Hera drove them mad, and Ino became the sea goddess Leucothea.
