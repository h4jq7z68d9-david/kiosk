Krater with Demeter and Triptolemos

Athenian, ca. 490 BCE

Red-figure volute krater · Badisches Landesmuseum, Karlsruhe

Made for: A large wine-mixing bowl; it was found in Sicily, an island famous for its grain and its worship of Demeter.

While searching for her daughter, Demeter came to Eleusis disguised as an old woman and rested by the Well of the Maiden. The royal family took her in, and in return she taught their son Triptolemus to farm. Here he sits in a winged chariot as Demeter sends him to spread grain across the world. Eleusis became the site of the Mysteries, secret rites that promised initiates a happier afterlife.

Eleusis
Town near Athens where Demeter was welcomed and where her Mysteries were held.
Well of the Maiden
The well at Eleusis where the grieving Demeter sat in disguise.
Eleusinian Mysteries
Secret rites for Demeter and Persephone; initiates were promised a better lot after death.
Mystery, initiate
A mystery is a secret rite revealed only to initiates (mystai), who swore never to tell.
Triptolemus
Eleusinian prince whom Demeter taught to farm and sent out in a winged chariot.
Mater Dolorosa
“Sorrowful Mother,” the image of the grieving Virgin Mary, often compared to the mourning Demeter.
