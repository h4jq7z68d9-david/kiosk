Brygos Painter

Cup with a maenad

Athenian, ca. 490–480 BCE

White-ground kylix (interior) · Staatliche Antikensammlungen, Munich

Made for: A luxury drinking cup; white-ground technique was fragile and costly, used for special pieces.

Maenads (“mad women”), also called Bacchae, were the female followers of Dionysus who roamed the mountains in ecstatic dances. This one swings a live leopard by the tail, holds a thyrsus, a fennel staff tipped with a pinecone, and wears a snake in her hair. The rites could end in sparagmos, tearing a living animal apart.

  • Maenads: Dionysus’s female followers
  • Thyrsus, leopard, snake
  • Ecstasy in the mountains
Bacchae, Thyiades, Maenads
Names for the frenzied women who worshipped Dionysus.
Thyrsus
A fennel staff topped with a pinecone and ivy, carried by Dionysus’s followers.
Enthousiasmos
Being filled with the god (“en-theos”), the root of “enthusiasm.”
Ekstasis
“Standing outside oneself,” the ecstatic state Dionysus’s rites produced.
