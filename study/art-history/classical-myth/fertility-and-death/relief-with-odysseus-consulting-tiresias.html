Relief with Odysseus consulting Tiresias

Roman, late 1st century CE

Marble · Louvre, Paris

Made for: A decorative relief for a Roman home or tomb, showing a famous scene from Homer.

In Odyssey 11, the witch Circe sends Odysseus to the edge of the world, the land of the Cimmerians, to consult the dead seer Tiresias. He digs a pit and pours in sacrificial blood, and the shades flock to drink it. Here Odysseus crouches by the pit as a shade approaches. Odysseus’s visit is an act of necromancy, calling up the dead to learn the future.

  • Circe sends Odysseus to the dead
  • Blood offering draws the shades
  • Tiresias foretells his journey home
Tiresias
The blind Theban prophet who keeps his wisdom even in death.
Circe
Witch-goddess who sends Odysseus to consult the dead.
Necromancer
One who calls up the dead to ask about the future.
Cimmerians
A people living in perpetual mist at the edge of the world, near the entrance to Hades.

Themes

  • A gloomy afterlife for almost everyone
