Exekias

Achilles and Ajax Playing a Game

c. 540–530 BCE

Black-figure terracotta amphora, 61.1 cm high · Vatican Museums, Vatican City

Exekias signed this amphora as both potter and painter. Its scene, found nowhere in Homer, shows the two greatest Greek warriors bent over a board game during the war. It was found at Vulci in Etruria and given to Pope Gregory XVI in 1834.

  • Words come from their mouths: “four” for Achilles, “three” for Ajax, so Achilles is winning.
  • Achilles still wears his helmet; Ajax has taken his off.
  • Very fine incised detail covers their cloaks.
  • On the other side, Castor and Pollux return home to their parents.

The subject

Achilles and Ajax were the strongest Greek fighters at Troy. Both died there: Achilles by Paris’s arrow, Ajax by his own hand.
