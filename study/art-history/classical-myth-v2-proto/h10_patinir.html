Joachim Patinir

Charon Crossing the Styx

1520–24

Oil on panel, 64 × 103 cm · Museo del Prado, Madrid

Patinir, a pioneer of landscape painting in Antwerp, turns the river of the dead into a moral choice. It probably belonged to Philip II and was saved from the Alcázar fire of 1734.

  • The picture divides into three bands: paradise with angels at left, the dark Styx in the middle, hell at right.
  • Cerberus guards the gate of hell on the right.
  • The small soul in Charon’s boat turns, in profile, toward the easy road to damnation.

The subject

Charon the ferryman carried the souls of the dead across the Styx for a coin. Patinir blends this pagan image with Christian heaven and hell.
