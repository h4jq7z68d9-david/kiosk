Egon Schiele

Portrait of Franz Hauer

1914

Drypoint, plate 13 × 11 cm. The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

A small printed head of Franz Hauer, a tavern owner who, in Jane Kallir's account, was the painter's most important collector from 1912 to 1914, buying nine of his oils.

  • Look at the looping, curled beard and the long parallel lines of the brow.
  • The head is built from line alone, darker wherever the lines crowd together.
  • The jittery line is left raw and open, giving the down-turned face a restless, searching look.
  • The Met holds several other 1914 drypoints by him, including a self-portrait and a portrait of Arthur Roessler.
  • This impression comes from a 1922 portfolio of his prints, part of the Met's Scofield Thayer bequest of 1982.
Drypoint
A print made by scratching lines into a metal plate with a sharp point, without acid.
