Ernst Ludwig Kirchner

English Step Dancers

1911

Colour woodcut, image 16 × 10 3/4 in. National Gallery of Art, Washington

The lines are cut in a wood block, so every contour is stiff and splintery, and the colour comes in three flat inks printed with the black.

Look at

  • The black lines are thick, straight-sided and broken, the marks of a knife in wood rather than a brush.
  • Pink, green and ochre are printed as flat areas with no shading, and the pink sits slightly off the black.
  • The dancers' legs are long and angular, with the stripes of their tights cut as parallel gouges.
  • The plant behind is simplified to a few cut leaves, each a single shape.
  • Printed by the artist himself, as the inscription says; given to the Jena art society in 1918, seized from it in 1937 as 'degenerate art', and in New York by 1940.
