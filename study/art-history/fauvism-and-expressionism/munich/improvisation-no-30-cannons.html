Wassily Kandinsky

Improvisation No. 30 (Cannons)

1913

Oil on canvas, 111 × 111.3 cm. Art Institute of Chicago, Chicago

A square canvas of swirling color in which a few objects can still be picked out. Its subtitle names the clearest of them.

  • First shown in London in July 1913, at the Allied Artists' Association salon in the Royal Albert Hall.
  • Look at the lower right for the cannons, then up at the leaning red and white buildings.
  • He told the collector Arthur Jerome Eddy the cannons probably reflected that year's war talk; the real content was what the forms and colors make us feel (Art Institute summary).
  • He called his improvisations of 1910–14 unconscious, spontaneous expressions; the guns show how much of the visible world could still slip in.
  • Eddy probably bought it from the artist; his family gave it to Chicago in 1931.
