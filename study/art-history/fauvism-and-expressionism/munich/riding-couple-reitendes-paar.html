Wassily Kandinsky

Riding Couple (Reitendes Paar)

1906–07

Oil on canvas, 55.4 × 50.7 cm. Städtische Galerie im Lenbachhaus und Kunstbau München, Munich

Two figures in Russian dress share a dappled horse among birch trunks, with a domed city like the Kremlin across the river. Scholar Vivian Endicott Barnett groups such Russian folk subjects with the work he made abroad.

  • Painted in winter 1906–07, while the artist lived at Sèvres, near Paris.
  • Look at the far bank: towers and onion domes glitter in a band of light above the dark water.
  • Small dabs of bright paint sit on a dark ground, like beads sewn on cloth. A tempera study and pencil sketches came first.
  • The museum connects it with Symbolism and Jugendstil, styles that put mood ahead of observation. The horse and rider, later a key motif in his art, already appear.
  • Left to Gabriele Münter by the artist, it was hers from 1926; she gave it to the museum in 1957.
