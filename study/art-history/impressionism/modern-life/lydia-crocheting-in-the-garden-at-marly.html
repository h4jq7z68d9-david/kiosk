Modern Life

Mary Cassatt

Lydia Crocheting in the Garden at Marly

1880

Oil on canvas, 65.6 × 92.6 cm. The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Cassatt's older sister Lydia sits crocheting in a sunlit walled garden, dressed far more formally than the task requires. Lydia was the relative Cassatt painted most often in these years.

  • One of Cassatt's few outdoor paintings, it catches the strong sunlight of a summer garden. Painting in the open air, in full daylight, was central to Impressionist practice.
  • Painted in summer 1880 at Marly-le-Roi, where the Cassatt family spent the season.
  • Hand needlework grew more prized in wealthy homes as factory-made textiles spread.
  • Look at the large, close figure set against the path and dark flower beds behind.
  • Mrs. Gardner Cassatt gave it to the Met in 1965.

Well-off Paris families often spent the summer in villages such as Marly-le-Roi. Needlework, reading and visits filled the days of women of Cassatt's class.
