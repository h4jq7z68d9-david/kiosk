Modern Life

Edgar Degas

Woman Bathing in a Shallow Tub

1885

Charcoal and pastel on light green wove paper, now discolored to warm gray, 81.3 × 56.2 cm. The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

A woman bends double over her lower leg in a wide, shallow tub, a cloth in her hand. Degas showed it among his pastel nudes at the last Impressionist exhibition, in 1886.

  • Her face is hidden and her body folds over the task, as if no one were watching. Impressionist painters often showed people absorbed in private, ordinary tasks like this.
  • Signed and dated "Degas / 85" at upper left.
  • Look at the tub, seen from above, nearly filling the bottom of the sheet.
  • Degas traded it to Mary Cassatt; it came to the Met with the Havemeyer bequest in 1929.

In the mid-1880s Degas made a series of pastels of women bathing and drying themselves. His bathers are ordinary women, not the goddesses or nymphs that usually justified a nude in art.
