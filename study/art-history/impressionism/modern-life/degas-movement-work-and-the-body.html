Modern Life

Degas: Movement, Work and the Body

Dancers, laundresses, jockeys and bathers, seen from odd angles.

Edgar Degas showed at nearly every Impressionist exhibition, but he worked mainly indoors, from drawings, models and memory. He returned to ballet, laundresses, racing and bathers for more than thirty years.

  • He sent work to the official Salon in the 1860s, then joined the independent exhibitions in 1874.
  • The Paris Opéra was his most frequent setting: first the old house, which burned in 1873, then the new Palais Garnier.
  • He used the group shows to test new kinds of work: a wax figure in 1881, pastel nudes in 1886.
  • Some works, such as the steeplechase scene and Four Dancers, stayed in his studio until his estate sales in 1918.
pastel
Sticks of powdered color, used like chalk; Degas often layered it over a print.
petit rat
Nickname for a pupil of the Paris Opéra ballet school.
