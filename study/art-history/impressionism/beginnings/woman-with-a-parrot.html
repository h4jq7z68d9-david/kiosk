Beginnings

Gustave Courbet

Woman with a Parrot

1866

Oil on canvas, 129.5 × 195.6 cm. The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

A life-size nude sprawled on a rumpled sheet, shown at the 1866 Salon by the leader of Realism. Courbet used the scale of a Salon nude but gave her no myth.

  • Courbet paints a real body, tangled hair and all, not an ideal one. The Impressionists took from Realism this demand to paint people as they are.
  • Several critics objected to the awkward pose.
  • The state briefly thought of buying it, but it stayed with Courbet until 1870.
  • Manet began his own woman with a parrot the same year.
  • Louisine Havemeyer's bequest brought it to the Met in 1929.

Courbet refused gods and heroes as subjects. Here he turned that stance on the nude, the academy's most valued subject.
