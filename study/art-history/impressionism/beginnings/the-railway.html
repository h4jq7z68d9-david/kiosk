Beginnings

Édouard Manet

The Railway

1873

Oil on canvas, 93.3 × 111.5 cm. National Gallery of Art, Washington

A young woman with a book and a puppy sits by an iron railing while a girl looks through at the steam below. Manet sent it to the Salon of 1874.

  • Manet kept to the official Salon and did not join the independent exhibition his friends opened in April 1874.
  • The setting is the rail yard leading into the Gare Saint-Lazare, beside Manet's new studio.
  • Manet never says whether the two are mother and daughter or nanny and child. Impressionist pictures likewise show a slice of modern life with no story attached.
  • The figures mirror each other: one faces us, the other turns away.
  • Horace Havemeyer bequeathed it to the National Gallery in 1956.

The Gare Saint-Lazare served the western suburbs and the Normandy coast. Monet and Caillebotte also painted its iron bridge, the Pont de l'Europe.
