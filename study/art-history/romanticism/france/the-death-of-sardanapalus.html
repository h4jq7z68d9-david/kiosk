France

Eugène Delacroix

The Death of Sardanapalus

1827

Oil on canvas, 392 × 496 cm. Musée du Louvre, Paris

A defeated Assyrian king watches from his bed as his servants kill his women and horses before his own death. Inspired by a play by Lord Byron, the picture caused a scandal.

  • A late addition to the Salon of 1827–28, it went on view in February 1828.
  • Look at the huge red bed, which slides diagonally down toward the viewer.
  • Delacroix worked on it in the second half of 1827, on canvas from the Haro firm; the Louvre restored it in 2022–23.
  • Bodies, jewels and animals tumble in every direction, with nothing to anchor the center. Christopher Riopelle of the National Gallery, London, in a 2016 lecture, describes the disorder as running through both the subject and the composition.
  • Unsold until 1846, it passed through several collections before the Louvre bought it in 1921.
