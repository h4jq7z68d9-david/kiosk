France

François Rude

The Departure of the Volunteers of 1792 (La Marseillaise)

1833–36

Stone, high relief. Arc de Triomphe de l'Étoile, Paris

A winged figure shouts and brandishes a sword above armed citizens rushing to defend France against foreign armies. It is one of four colossal groups carved for the piers of the Arc de Triomphe.

  • Commissioned under Adolphe Thiers as a political choice of the July Monarchy; the arch was inaugurated in 1836.
  • Look below her: a bearded veteran raises his arm in a rallying gesture and hugs a naked young recruit to his side, with an elder at the back offering counsel.
  • Ancient-style armor and nudity replace 1792 uniforms, so that the struggle reads as universal, not local.
  • Public sculpture gains a Romantic voice: violent motion and raw emotion take the place of the calm, balanced poses expected of official monuments.
  • It remains on the arch, which the Centre des monuments nationaux now manages.
