France

Eugène Delacroix

The Convulsionists of Tangier

1837–38

Oil on canvas, 95.57 × 128.59 cm. Minneapolis Institute of Art, Minneapolis

Members of the Aissawa, a Sufi brotherhood, surge through a street in Tangier in a religious trance while townspeople look on. Here North Africa appears as a crowd in motion, not a quiet interior.

  • During the 1832 mission, he and the Comte de Mornay watched the procession pass from a boarded-up window under the roof.
  • Look at the raised arms and bowed heads at the center, and the onlookers crowding the rooftops above.
  • Painted in France about five years after the event.
  • Riopelle (National Gallery) notes that in Morocco Delacroix made only drawings and watercolors; the paintings came later, from memory, and here he heightened the color of the scene he had glimpsed.
  • Long known as The Fanatics of Tangier, it was once owned by James J. Hill and came to Mia as a bequest of J. Jerome Hill.
