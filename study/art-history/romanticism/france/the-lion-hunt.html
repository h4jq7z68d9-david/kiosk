France

Eugène Delacroix

The Lion Hunt

1860–61

Oil on canvas, 76.5 × 98.5 cm. Art Institute of Chicago, Chicago

Hunters on horseback and on foot attack two lions in hilly, grassy country under a heavy, cloudy sky. It is one of several hunt scenes Delacroix returned to in his last years.

  • First exhibited after his death, at the 1885 Delacroix exhibition at the École des Beaux-Arts, Paris.
  • Look at the lion leaping onto a fallen horse at the left and the second lion roaring over the men beneath it.
  • The scene is invented: having never watched such a hunt, he assembled it from earlier studies of terrain, Islamic dress and lions at the zoo.
  • Strokes stay loose and visible even in manes and faces, so men, horses and lions blur into one tangled mass. The quick handling adds to the violence.
  • Bought by Mr. and Mrs. Potter Palmer in 1892 and bequeathed to the museum in 1922.
