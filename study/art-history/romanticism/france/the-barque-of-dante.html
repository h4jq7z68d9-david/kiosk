France

Eugène Delacroix

The Barque of Dante

1822

Oil on canvas, 189 × 241.5 cm. Musée du Louvre, Paris

Two poets cross a river in hell in a small boat while the damned claw at its sides. The National Gallery's Christopher Riopelle says this first Salon picture made its young painter famous almost overnight.

  • Shown at the Salon of 1822 and bought from it by the royal museums for 2,000 francs.
  • Look at Dante in his red hood, recoiling, while a calm Virgil steadies him by the hand.
  • The crowded, tilting boat on dark water owes a clear debt to Géricault's raft, shown three years before.
  • Literature becomes a source: a scene from Dante's Inferno is painted with Rubens-like color and heavy, twisting muscle instead of the cool, drawn line taught in David's school.
  • Displayed at the Luxembourg for as long as Delacroix lived; transferred to the Louvre in 1874.
