France

Théodore Géricault

The Raft of the Medusa

1818–19

Oil on canvas, 491 × 716 cm. Musée du Louvre, Paris

Survivors of a French navy shipwreck crowd a makeshift raft and strain toward a sail on the horizon. Of about 150 people put on the raft in 1816, fifteen were alive when rescued.

  • First shown at the Salon of 1819 under the neutral title Scène de naufrage (Shipwreck Scene).
  • Look at the bodies: they rise in a pyramid from the dead at the front to a man waving a cloth.
  • Begun in a rented Paris studio in 1818 and finished in a theater foyer in July 1819.
  • No king, saint or general appears: a recent disaster and its anonymous victims are given the size of history painting, the most prestigious kind of art.
  • Shown to paying crowds in London and Dublin in 1820–21, then bought for the Crown at his estate sale in 1824.
