Revolution and Nightmare

Henry Fuseli

The Nightmare

1781

Oil on canvas, 101.7 × 127.1 cm. Detroit Institute of Arts, Detroit

A sleeping woman lies sprawled across a bed while a demon squats on her chest and a horse's head pushes through the curtains. The picture made the Swiss-born painter's name in London.

  • Shown at the Royal Academy in 1782. In a 2022 lecture, Martin Myrone of the Paul Mellon Centre notes that, unusually for Fuseli, it cited no poem or play.
  • Look at the woman's head and arm, hanging limp off the edge of the bed toward the floor.
  • Myrone links the curtained room to fashionable bedroom scenes by painters such as Jean-Baptiste Le Prince; the demon was Fuseli's twist.
  • The theme is the sleeping mind. Putting dreams and irrational fear on a public exhibition wall was a bold step that made the inner life a fit subject for painting.
  • First owned by Sir Brooke Boothby; the Detroit Institute of Arts bought it in 1955.
Royal Academy
Britain's royal academy of art, founded in London in 1768, with a yearly exhibition and a school; a separate body from the Society of Artists.
