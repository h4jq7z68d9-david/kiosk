Revolution and Nightmare

Samuel Palmer

The Harvest Moon

c. 1833

Oil on paper laid on panel, 22.2 × 27.6 cm. Yale Center for British Art, New Haven

Farm workers gather and bundle sheaves of grain late into the night under a full moon and a scatter of stars. Palmer made this small picture near the end of his years in the Kent village of Shoreham.

  • Harvesting by night was common practice at the time, the museum notes, so the scene itself is not invented.
  • Look at the workers: they show no strain, and seem to move in step, almost dancing.
  • The oil is laid on in small, thick touches, and the moon, high among the trees at upper left, washes the field in glowing orange-gold.
  • The countryside becomes a vision of plenty. For Palmer the natural world was a sign of God's handiwork, so an ordinary English farm gains an almost sacred calm.
