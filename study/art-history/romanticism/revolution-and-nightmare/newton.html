Revolution and Nightmare

William Blake

Newton

1795–c. 1805

Color print, ink and watercolor on paper, 46 × 60 cm. Tate, London

A muscular nude scientist sits hunched on a rock, drawing with compasses on a sheet at his feet. Blake turns the hero of the Enlightenment into a man shut inside his own diagram.

  • First shown in public at the Burlington Fine Arts Club, London, in 1876, long after Blake's death.
  • Look at the murky green setting and the patterned rock, which the Tate catalogue reads as a scene under water.
  • The pose comes from Michelangelo's Abias in the Sistine Chapel, which Blake knew from an engraving.
  • Blake's attack on Newton is an early sign of a lasting Romantic distrust of science that reduces the living world to measurement.
  • Presented to the Tate by W. Graham Robertson in 1939.
