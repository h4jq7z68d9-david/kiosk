Landscape and the Sublime

J.M.W. Turner

Snow Storm: Hannibal and his Army Crossing the Alps

exhibited 1812

Oil on canvas, 146 × 237.5 cm. Tate Britain, London

A dark storm cloud curls over the Carthaginian army as it struggles through a mountain pass. The soldiers are tiny, and the weather, not the general, fills most of the canvas.

  • At the Royal Academy the painter insisted it hang low on the wall and threatened to withdraw it otherwise.
  • Look at the small orange sun, caught inside the dark arc of cloud.
  • The catalogue carried verses credited to his own poem Fallacies of Hope, the first time he did this; the poem was probably never finished.
  • Here the sublime comes from weather, a force no general can command or escape. Critics at the exhibition already praised the picture in exactly those terms.
  • Part of the Turner Bequest, the paintings he left to the nation, accepted in 1856; at Tate since 1910.
