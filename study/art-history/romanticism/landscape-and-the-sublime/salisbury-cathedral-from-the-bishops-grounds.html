Landscape and the Sublime

John Constable

Salisbury Cathedral from the Bishop's Grounds

ca. 1825

Oil on canvas, 87.9 × 111.8 cm. The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

This is the Met's full-scale study, the same size as the finished picture of 1826 now in the Frick Collection, New York. The design exists in several versions; this one shows the painter working it out.

  • The finished picture was made for John Fisher, Bishop of Salisbury, uncle of the painter's close friend Archdeacon John Fisher; the commission goes back to 1822.
  • Look at the small figure of the bishop at lower left, gesturing toward the spire.
  • As he developed the design, he opened up the canopy of trees and brightened the sky around the spire.
  • Tall trees arch over the scene and frame the cathedral like a gateway, so nature sets the stage for the medieval building rather than serving as background.
  • Bequeathed to the Met by Mary Stillman Harkness in 1950.
