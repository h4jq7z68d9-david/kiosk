Landscape and the Sublime

John Constable

Hadleigh Castle, The Mouth of the Thames—Morning after a Stormy Night

1829

Oil on canvas, 121.9 × 164.5 cm. Yale Center for British Art, New Haven

Two shattered towers of a medieval castle stand on a hill above a wide estuary, under clouds still heavy with rain. Light breaks over the distant water, but the foreground stays dark and broken.

  • Shown at the Royal Academy, his first show there after election as a full Academician that February.
  • Look at the figure and dog at lower left, small beside the crumbling stone, and a second figure with cows on the slope beyond.
  • One of his six-footers; he worked out the design first in a full-size study, now at Tate Britain.
  • Painted in the months after his wife Maria died in November 1828, and its bleak mood is often linked to that grief. A real place becomes a picture of feeling.
  • Sold from his studio after his death; Paul Mellon bought it in 1961 and gave it to Yale in 1977.
