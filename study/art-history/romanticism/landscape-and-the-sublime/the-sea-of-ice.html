Landscape and the Sublime

Caspar David Friedrich

The Sea of Ice

1823–24

Oil on canvas, 96.7 × 126.9 cm. Hamburger Kunsthalle, Hamburg

Slabs of broken ice pile up into a jagged pyramid under a pale sky. The museum reads the clearing at the top as a hint of hope, or of something beyond this world.

  • Once called The Wreck of Hope, a name taken from a ship in a lost earlier version.
  • Look at the right: the dark hull of a crushed ship lies tipped on its side, half swallowed by the ice.
  • Built up from three small oil sketches of ice breaking up on the Elbe in winter 1820–21, all now in Hamburg.
  • Nature's force overwhelms human effort, and the ice is heaped up like a monument. The museum names William Edward Parry's Arctic voyages as a possible source and notes readings from religious to political.
  • Acquired by the Kunsthalle in 1905, the same year as one of the ice sketches.
