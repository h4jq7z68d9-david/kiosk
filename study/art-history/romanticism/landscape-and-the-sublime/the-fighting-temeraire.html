Landscape and the Sublime

J.M.W. Turner

The Fighting Temeraire

1839

Oil on canvas, 90.7 × 121.6 cm. The National Gallery, London

A pale warship that fought at Trafalgar in 1805 is towed by a small dark steam tug to a shipbreaker's yard. The ship had been sold for scrap in 1838.

  • He most likely never saw the tow and worked from reports; the picture went to the Royal Academy the next year.
  • Look at the old ship, the palest shape in the picture, behind the dark tug, with the sunset blazing at right.
  • In a 2016 gallery talk, the National Gallery's Matthew Morgan points out that the water holds almost no blue and that the sunset paint is thick and heavily worked.
  • Critics then read it as a lament for the age of sail. Morgan (National Gallery) notes the painter had drawn steamboats with interest since 1815 and treats the meaning as open.
  • He kept it until his death and called it his darling.
