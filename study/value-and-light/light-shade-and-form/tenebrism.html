Light, Shade and Form

Tenebrism

Tenebrism, from the Latin tenebrae, "darkness", is chiaroscuro at its most extreme: brightly lit figures set against near-total darkness, with little in between.

Look at

  • A single beam enters high at the right, from beyond the frame, and slants down across the seated men.
  • The painted window above lets in only a dim, diffused glow; the beam is what picks out the figures.

Artemisia Gentileschi

Judith and Her Maidservant with the Head of Holofernes, about 1623–25

A lone candle lights the room; Judith lifts a hand near it as though shading it, and the maid kneeling below catches the same light.

The Detroit Institute of Arts links this painting's theatrical lighting to Caravaggio.
