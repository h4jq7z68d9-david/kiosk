Light, Shade and Form

Local Value and Light

Every surface has its own lightness, its local value; the light falling on it then adds lights and shadows on top.

Look at

  • The two balls share one light, and each turns from a lit side to a shadow side in the same places.
  • On the darker ball the whole pattern is shifted down and squeezed into a narrower range, so its shine stands out more.

Francisco de Zurbarán

Still Life with Lemons, Oranges and a Rose, 1633

Light from the left strikes pale lemons, darker oranges and a white cup, and each holds on to its own lightness whether lit or shaded.

Cross noted that the lit side of a dark object can look lighter than the shaded side of a light one.
