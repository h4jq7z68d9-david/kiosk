Light, Shade and Form

Chiaroscuro

Chiaroscuro (Italian for "light-dark") is the balance and pattern of light and shade across a whole picture, arranged as one design.

Look at

  • Only a few things catch the light: the face, the pale sleeve, the hand and the bust it rests on, while everything else drops into brown gloom.
  • A band of shadow runs over the upper face and keeps the eyes partly veiled.

Leonardo da Vinci

The Virgin of the Rocks, about 1491/2–9 and 1506–8

The figures come out of the dark grotto by soft degrees, with no hard outline at the Virgin's temples or nose, an effect later called sfumato, from the Italian for "to turn to smoke".

Leonardo built these figures over an underpainting in black and white, so their values were set before color went on.
