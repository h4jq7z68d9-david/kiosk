Canvas

Canvas is a tough cloth, usually linen, pulled tight over a wooden frame.

It weighs less than wood and can be rolled up. Venice switched to canvas from panels and walls between about 1450 and 1550; damp air and the sailcloth trade are the usual reasons given, though recent research doubts any single one.

  • Seen from the back: a crossbar and corner braces keep the frame square.
  • Until the 1800s, big canvases were usually sewn from strips; the Titian on the next page is an exception.
