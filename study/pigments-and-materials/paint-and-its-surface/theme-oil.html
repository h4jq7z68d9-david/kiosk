Oil

Oil paint binds pigment in an oil that dries, usually linseed; it hardens slowly as it takes up oxygen from the air.

Slow drying gives the painter time: colors can be blended on the surface, laid on thick or put on as glazes.

  • Stradanus's print (about 1600) repeats Vasari's legend that Van Eyck invented oil; it was used centuries earlier.
  • Oil then moved south; Giovanni Bellini used it in Venice by the 1460s.
