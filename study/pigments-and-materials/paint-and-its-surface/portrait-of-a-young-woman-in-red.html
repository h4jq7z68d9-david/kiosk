Paint and Its Surface

Roman Egypt

Portrait of a young woman in red

90–120 CE

Encaustic on limewood with gold leaf, 38.1 × 18.4 cm. The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

A Met study (published by the Getty) identifies the paint as pigment in beeswax, laid straight onto a sliver of linden wood only millimeters thick, with no ground.

  • Its colors: lead white, iron ochres, a see-through red lake for the garment and gold leaf; a copper pigment darkens the shadows of the face.
  • Look around the nose and under the lower lip: parallel marks left by a heated tool.
