Earth and Fire: The First Palettes

The first palettes held what could be dug up or charred: red and yellow iron earths, black charcoal and white chalk.

Fire also made Egyptian blue, at right, the oldest known synthetic pigment: a new compound cooked in a furnace, not dug up. It is very stable, though a binder that yellows around it changes how it looks.

  • At Blombos Cave in South Africa, a kit for mixing red ochre paint is about 100,000 years old.
  • Egyptian blue fell out of use in the Middle Ages; Raphael's Galatea fresco (1511–12) is a rare late use.
