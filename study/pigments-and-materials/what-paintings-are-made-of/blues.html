What Paintings Are Made Of

Blues

Before 1700 a painter could choose from only a few blues, and two of them were crushed minerals.

Look at

  • Left: lapis lazuli, which yields ultramarine; powdered on its own it comes out grayish, so painters worked the blue out of a paste of wax and resin.
  • Right: azurite, a copper ore with darker, blocky crystals; its green patches are a reminder that azurite can turn green.

Johannes Vermeer

Girl with a Pearl Earring, about 1665

The blue of the turban is top-quality natural ultramarine, lightened with lead white in the light and mixed with chalk in the shade.

In fifteenth-century Florence the best ultramarine might cost as much as gold leaf, so painters usually kept it for the most important figure.
